# Keep the Core, Build the Exam Layer

Toishi's daily loop already has nearly every practice mechanic that the best-evidenced systems share. It gates new skills behind a learning phase before review. It keeps Recall separate from a Level that changes only on evidence. It runs spaced cumulative checks, routes each error to the prerequisite behind the failed step without lowering the bar, charges for hints, explains every answer, mixes review without labels and places students with a check. The remaining gains lie elsewhere. Even the strongest platforms move outcomes only modestly: ASSISTments gained **+0.10 SD** in a 9,073-student replication, and Khan Academy with its AI tutor gained **+0.06–0.08 SD** per school year. Newer learning science lowers expectations further. In mathematics alone, spacing is worth **g = 0.28**, and testing versus restudy is worth **g = 0.18** with a confidence interval that crosses zero. Two large classroom experiments on angle relations found no interleaving effect at all. The same evidence says how to set the mastery bar: count clean successes on separate days, and make the in-session gate adaptive. A randomized ALEKS test found that a higher threshold bought **under 0.02** of retention for about 1.5 extra minutes per topic. The two biggest gaps are where exam candidates and question banks invest most: gaokao, suneung, Japanese 赤本 (red-book past papers) and JEE students, and USMLE banks. The first is a mistake lifecycle that ends in retirement instead of a growing list. The second is an exam-rehearsal layer. It builds papers from a blueprint, stages them from untimed to shortened-time, analyzes them by lost points and attempt quality, and reports a score range checked against real results. For a gaokao candidate, the app can become the strongest *method* layer available. No Chinese, Korean or Japanese tool examined runs a parameterized spaced scheduler. It cannot offer gaokao *content*, because it is English-only and limited to openly licensed problems. Four backlog bugs switch off proven mechanics today, and they come first. Verification is already strong, with 4,144 core tests and independent reviews that found 74 false accepts in the checker; what is missing is checks that agents cannot argue their way past, traceability from each requirement to its tests, a hazard table ranked by severity, and a validation phase honest about one learner's statistics, since detecting a five-point retention difference takes about **1,100–1,400 rated reviews**, roughly one experiment per semester.

## Sixteen proven mechanics are already in the design, and four bugs switch some off

Side by side, about twenty practice platforms converge on a short list of mechanics. They also share a principle Math Academy states outright: remediation "keeps the bar where it's at." Help comes as more questions, a break spent on other topics, targeted reviews of the key prerequisite behind the failed step, and a retake with different questions. It never comes as hints that reveal the answer ([Math Academy Way, pp. 300–302](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)). ALEKS separates provisional learning from confirmed mastery because new learning is fragile. Across 8.4 million retention events, newly learned items at the bottom of its prerequisite structure leveled off near **60%** retained, and items deep in it near **80%** ([ALEKS technical paper](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/research/prek-12/essa-tier-4/aleks-practical-perspective-knowledge-space-theory.pdf)). ASSISTments counts a skill builder done after three correct in a row. If a student can't finish within ten questions, it stops and says "try again tomorrow." Seven days after completion it reassesses with one random question, then again at 14, 28 and 56 days ([ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org/blog-posts/how-assistments-skillbuilders-works-in-my-class-and-why-it-will-work-for-you-too)). UWorld and AMBOSS split a learning mode with instant explanations from an exam mode that holds feedback until the end ([UWorld](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/features/); [AMBOSS](https://support.amboss.com/hc/en-us/articles/45511273700369)). Toishi's spec already contains each of these, usually in a stricter or better-measured form. So this report mentions them only briefly and spends its length elsewhere.

The best platforms' outcomes set realistic expectations. ASSISTments has the strongest homework-practice evidence found: a 63-school replication with 9,073 seventh graders gained **+0.10 SD** on the state test a year later, against 0.18 SD in the original Maine trial ([Arnold Ventures](https://www.arnoldventures.org/stories/a-replication-randomized-controlled-trial-rct-of-assistments-an-online-study-tool-to-improve-student-math-outcomes-in-seventh-grade)). A two-year cluster RCT of Khan Academy with its Khanmigo AI tutor found **+0.06–0.08 SD per school year**, the same as Khan practice without AI. Students messaged the tutor in only **17%** of the exercise sessions where they made a mistake ([NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)). RAND's trial of Cognitive Tutor found nothing in year one and roughly double the control group's gains for high schools in year two ([EdSurge](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2013-08-27-the-nature-of-big-edtech-research)). The What Works Clearinghouse rates the same study inconsistently ([WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/study/82101)). A 2025 meta-analysis of K-12 tutoring systems (a preprint) pooled **g = 0.271**. Use as homework reached 0.317, while after-school use reached only **0.014** ([arXiv 2511.04997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04997)). Whole-package effects of 0.1–0.3 SD are what the best tools achieve. How the tool is used matters as much as any single feature: a daily replacement for homework is the context with evidence, and an occasional extra is not.

Every table in this report grades evidence on one scale. **Strong** means meta-analyses or replicated randomized trials, **Moderate** one large randomized study or several good smaller ones, and **Weak** small, lab-only or correlational work. **Convergent practice** means many independent products or practitioners do it with no outcome test, and **Vendor** means a company's own unverified claim. Recommended mechanics are numbered M1–M24 and engineering items E1–E8.

The hand test on 2026-11-01 found four defects, and each one switches off a mechanism in this table. In backlog #3, a two-problem pool re-served the problem whose solution had just been shown, which turns fresh-problem retrieval into recognition. In #4, no worked example appeared before a new skill's first problem, which removes the acquisition step. In #6, the "What went wrong?" picker opens below the fold and looks stuck, and the Khanmigo trial shows how quickly help that isn't in view gets ignored. In #1, Give up is labeled a confident error, which misreports the confidence signal. These four lead the must list, because no new mechanic is worth more than the ones they disable.

## Newer evidence shrinks expected gains and moves the mastery bar across days

The headline effect sizes in learning science come from pooled lab and classroom work, short delays and surface-level tests. Math-specific numbers are smaller. A 2025 meta-analysis of mathematics alone found spacing worth **g = 0.28** across 27 studies, 0.24 when embedded in real courses. It found testing versus restudy worth only **g = 0.18** across 7 studies, with a confidence interval that includes zero ([Murray et al. 2025](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/229807)). Interleaving, the earlier report's strongest single result (d = 0.83 in seventh-grade classrooms), now faces a null. Two large classroom experiments on angle relations "repeatedly failed to detect an interleaving/blocking effect" ([Rowlandson & Simpson](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/2979661)). A preregistered replication with 3,780 students in 189 classrooms will not report until 2027 ([IES](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/systematic-replication-study-interleaved-mathematics-practice)). Hattie's synthesis puts interleaving at d = 0.47, but from only **972 participants**, against 152,952 behind distributed practice. It also notes that 93% of all outcomes measured were surface-level ([Donoghue & Hattie 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feduc.2021.581216/pdf)). Elaborated feedback holds up best, with larger effects in mathematics than in other subjects ([Van der Kleij 2015](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314564881)). Plan on a stack of small-to-moderate effects that will not simply add up: a few percentage points per feature on a delayed exam. Adherence decides whether the stack is realized at all.

Successive relearning shows where the mastery bar belongs. Across three experiments with 533 students and more than 100,000 scored responses, Rawson and Dunlosky found the best recipe was 3 correct recalls in the first session, then 1 correct recall in each of about 3 widely spaced sessions. The two factors were subadditive: a high first-session criterion mattered a lot only when nothing followed it ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956)). One correct recall in each of three spaced sessions produced **more than twice** the recall of three correct in one session ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/teaching-current-directions-studying-psychopathology)). In a 212-student classroom field study, relearning to just one correct per session beat matched restudy, .55 to .41 (d = 0.705). Transfer to new items, however, was only marginal ([Higham et al.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/449677/1/Higham_et_al._in_press_JEdPsych.pdf)). Raising the in-session bar buys little. ALEKS randomized new topics to a high or low threshold across 806,279 students. The high threshold cost about **1.5 extra minutes per topic** (6.6 vs 5.1). Retention differences were mostly **under 0.02** and narrowed over weeks. They reached 0.03–0.04 only for students who struggled first, and gains concentrated in hard-to-retain topics (r = −0.86) ([Matayoshi et al. 2025](https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/download/900/254)). Model comparisons agree that "the choice of data sources for the mastery decision and the setting of thresholds matter more than the choice of learner modeling technique" ([Pelánek & Řihák 2017](https://www.ics.muni.cz/en/research/publications/1392193)). Toishi already applies the across-days half of this: Exam-level needs successes on 2 different days, and Mastered needs 3. The in-session half is a fixed 3-in-a-row gate, and the evidence says to make it adaptive (M1).

Fresh problems are the design's central bet, and two findings set its limits. Higham's benefits were concentrated on repeated items. Varied surface stories helped concept learning only after verbal pretraining: gains of 17.4% versus 7.0% with pretraining, and no benefit without it ([Braithwaite & Goldstone](https://pc.cogs.indiana.edu/papers/variationpreparation.pdf)). So variation belongs after a skill is learned, not during acquisition. Number-only template variants carry an untested risk that students memorize the pattern rather than learn the skill. The app should measure that risk rather than assume it away (M2). On wheel-spinning, under the common "3 in a row within 10" definition, 0.56% to 10.2% of student–skill pairs never reach mastery, depending on the dataset. A Bayesian definition agreed with that rule less than half the time ([Zhang et al. 2019](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED594575.pdf)). The cutoff is a design choice; giving the student a way out is not optional. Math Academy halts a failed lesson and reports an 80% pass on the retry without extra help. A second failure at the same worked example triggers reviews of the key prerequisites ([Math Academy Way, FAQ](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)). Toishi restarts a failed learning phase the next day with no limit on repeats (M3).

The contested interleaving evidence argues for keeping the mixed queue and measuring it, not for more mixing. When students order their own study, they rely mainly on blocked practice ([Onan et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/c835e588b7d24a2bb70eb40af0a640d1)), so the app should keep control of the queue. Confusable-pair placement is the cheap part with the most plausible mechanism, and the app can log whether it helps this learner (M4). On feedback, two boundary findings refine "always show the full solution." Feedback after a correct but low-confidence answer doubles its retention ([Butler 2008](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/correcting-a-metacognitive-error-feedback-increases-retention-of-/)). But even minimal feedback lowered scores, 62% versus 81%, for children who had already been taught the right strategy ([Fyfe & Rittle-Johnson](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/files/FyfeRittleJohnson2015_JEP.pdf)). Asking for confidence ratings during practice reversed the retrieval-over-restudy advantage in one preregistered study ([Educational Psychology Review 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2)). That supports keeping "unsure" a voluntary keystroke, as it is now, and never a required rating. High-confidence errors are corrected well in real school mathematics ([Foster et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10649-021-10084-7)), which the design's guaranteed next-day redo already uses. The open refinement is a shorter confirmation for sure, on-target answers on stable skills (M5).

Speed is the one place where the new evidence pulls against the earlier reports. Direct evidence for timed practice is almost entirely elementary basic facts. About 120 seconds of daily timed practice was the minimum effective dose, and gains did not carry over to related facts ([Peltier summary of Duhon 2022 and Poncy 2010](https://coreypeltier.substack.com/p/timed-math-practice)). A review of 19 studies found time pressure "generally acts as a stressor, causing suboptimal strategy selection." Its interaction with math anxiety was inconsistent ([Caviola et al. 2017](https://research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/3255605/1/Caviola_etal2017Frontiers.pdf)). Overt versus covert timing made no difference to anxiety in 113 fourth and fifth graders ([EdWeek on Maki et al.](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/do-timed-tasks-really-worsen-math-anxiety/2024/08)). No controlled college study shows timed drills improving multi-step exam scores. Math Academy's strong automaticity claims cite no studies, and its planned rapid-fire trainer had not shipped ([Skycak](https://justinmath.com/yes-you-need-to-be-automatic-on-math-facts/)). The current design already keeps time out of FSRS ratings except for the slow-and-past-target rule. But ENG-13 builds a target time from 70% of the student's own early median even when no timed exam exists, and Exam-level and Mastered both require meeting it. That makes pace a mastery gate with no evidence behind it outside a timed exam, so M6 ties the pace condition to an entered exam length. Fact fluency for genuinely retrieval-like content (trig values, derivative tables) is better served by short, optional sprints (M7). Khan's own A/B tests found that surfacing unmastered prerequisites before harder problems raised next-item correctness by 2.7% ([Khan Academy](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=21314)). That is a vendor result with a small effect, but cheap to copy (M8).

## Learners quit at the return from a break, not in the routine

Motivation research points away from rewards for a one-person tool. A meta-analysis of 344 samples (223,209 participants) found external regulation (reward and punishment) "unrelated to performance or persistence." Personal value was "particularly highly related to persistence" ([Howard et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620966789)). The newest broad gamification meta-analysis reports g = 0.822, but its text and tables disagree with each other ([Frontiers 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591086)). Math Academy needs XP penalties for an adversary this app doesn't have. Without penalties, some students submitted random guesses to fail out of hard tasks. After penalties were added, those students' pass rates rose from under 50% to over 90% (internal figures) ([Math Academy Way, pp. 313–314](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)).

The documented point where people quit is the return. WaniKani users describe coming back after 3–6 months to 600–1,600 reviews, and that "seeing the number killed motivation" ([WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/tips-for-recovering-from-burnout/54043)). An Anki forum proposal calls the wall of cards after a few missed days the main churn trigger ([Anki forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/proposal-backlog-recovery-planner-to-prevent-user-churn-burnout/70487)). Duolingo concedes that losing a streak after one missed day can lower motivation and push learners to avoid challenges to protect it. Its two-freeze allowance raised daily active learners by 0.38% without increasing skipping (company data) ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)). The strongest independent test, a Peru RCT with about 60,000 students, raised platform use with streak highlighting. Its learning result rests on the roughly 1,500 who took the endline test ([NBER w34173](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34173)).

The one causal result on practice volume in exam prep is cheap to copy. A 30-minute written plan (the outcome, obstacles, and if-then plans for where and when to work) led 35 students to complete about **140 PSAT practice questions** over a summer, against **84** for 31 controls (β = 0.27, p = .04) ([Duckworth et al. 2011](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/duckworth_self-regulation_11.pdf)). Showing students their own delayed-test results kept them using retrieval practice; general instruction about the testing effect did not ([Hui et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-021-09604-x)). The design already avoids overdue counts, triages quietly and spec'd a forgiving streak (STA-1, not yet built). It lacks the plan, the return experience and the own-data contrast.

## Exam candidates in four countries close a mistake loop the app leaves half open

Top scorers in four countries describe the same mistake discipline, though none of it has been tested in a controlled study. A 2026 gaokao candidate who scored 694 classifies each mistake on entry. Problems where her thinking got stuck go in the notebook. Careless slips get a one-line summary on the first page, skimmed before exams. She redoes the batch weekly with the solution covered and treats mock exams as a way to find weak sections, not as a verdict ([Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260821A0A5UZ00)). Eight Peking and Tsinghua admits disagree with each other about notebook size. One keeps it "as thin as possible" by consolidating entries. One says it cost more time than it returned ([The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16072260)). A Korean student with a perfect suneung math score labels each entry by cause: fully understood, a simple calculation mistake, didn't know the concept, or couldn't apply a known concept ([Donghwa News](https://www.dhnews.co.kr/news/view/179512497334451)). JEE Advanced 2023's top ranker spent 2–3 hours analyzing each mock paper, sorting errors into conceptual, calculation and silly ([CollegeDekho](https://www.collegedekho.com/amp/articles/jee-advanced-2026-final-45-day-mock-test-and-toppers-revision-strategy/)). Japan's 赤本 publisher has students repeat each major past-paper question under time until it earns three ○ marks for smooth solves ([Kyogakusha guide](https://akahon.net/doc/howto_2012.pdf)). The failure modes are just as consistent. Hand-copying one math problem took one student more than ten minutes, over six hours a week ([Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/read/cv50578712)). A notebook that is never re-tested becomes a "regret notebook" (后悔本) ([NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L45VFT0V0556FBHS.html)).

The tools built around these habits share one loop: capture the mistake, tag its knowledge point, push similar problems ("learn one, master the class"), re-grade, update. Youdao's device splits mistakes into "to be corrected" and "corrected" ([SMZDM review](https://post.smzdm.com/p/a5oxrl83/)). 错题Plus (Mistakes Plus) pushes a graded review paper and re-pushes from the results ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id1476015217)). 错题打印机 (Mistake Printer) assembles a printable paper from selected mistakes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/%E9%94%99%E9%A2%98%E6%89%93%E5%8D%B0%E6%9C%BA-%E8%87%AA%E5%8A%A8%E6%B8%85%E9%99%A4%E6%89%8B%E5%86%99%E7%AC%94%E8%BF%B9-%E8%AF%95%E5%8D%B7%E9%94%99%E9%A2%98%E6%95%B4%E7%90%86/id1598835335)). Korea's EBSi shows "my top 5 weaknesses" and builds a test paper from only the weak parts ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/ko/apps/ebsi/669006959)). Xueersi's 2025 tutor grades each handwritten step in real time and gives hints, not answers ([QbitAI](https://www.qbitai.com/2025/11/350840.html)). Two caveats limit what to borrow. The only scheduling found was "Ebbinghaus forgetting curve" reminders (伴鱼, Banyu) and Monoxer's undisclosed forgetting model; no parameterized scheduler like FSRS appeared anywhere ([China.com](https://m.tech.china.com/articles/20250701/202507011693608.html); [Resemom](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20260402/177068.html)). Quality is also weak. One parent said a learning tablet's essay scanning worked about half the time, and a Gankao contractor reported video open rates under 20% ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/china-student-tablet-ai/)).

Toishi already exceeds these tools on the analysis half of the loop. It has step-level blame, a check that confirms the blamed skill, and known wrong answers coded from common misconceptions. It is thin on the bookkeeping half. A missed problem is redone once (the next day if the student was sure, 3 days later if unsure). A planned "My mistakes" list allows unrated redos. STA-8 counts error types. What's missing is a state that tells the student whether a mistake is closed, a rule that retires it, and a way to drill from the list. The design principle comes from the earlier audit: a miss stays a miss and FSRS writes the schedule. So the lifecycle must be derived from events the app already logs, never run as a second scheduler (M12).

## Exam rehearsal needs a blueprint, a ladder and a score checked against reality

Evidence on mock exams is observational, but consistent. College Board matched students on background and prior achievement. Completing 1, 2 or 3+ full practice tests was associated with scores **25.7, 45.5 and 61.4** points higher, with larger gains for lower scorers. The authors state that "estimates are not causal" ([College Board 2025](https://research.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/DigitalSATPracticeTests_052025.pdf)). In a 399-person USMLE survey, the best single practice test explained about two thirds of score variance (R² 0.68). The average gap between best practice test and real score was 6.9 points, and six to nine tests looked optimal ([Seal et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198101)). Volume adds slowly: about 2 points per 1,000 bank questions after adjustment ([Deng et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4673073)). The serious platforms therefore keep two instruments apart. AMBOSS uses a Rasch knowledge profile to pick practice. A separate score predictor uses only practice exams, and it reported a mean absolute error of 8.52 points against 7,244 self-reported scores ([AMBOSS](https://blog.amboss.com/us/knowledge-profile)). Math Academy makes its quizzes deliberately harder than classroom quizzes: "timed, fully interleaved, and some forgetting has set in". Every missed question queues an immediate review ([Math Academy Way, FAQ](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)). Toishi's practice tests start well: exam-tier problems weighted toward low recall, one try, graded at the end, unanswered problems unrated. They stop short in four places. They have no blueprint, no staged ladder, no post-test analysis beyond reviews, and no comparison of predicted against real scores.

Asian exam ecosystems supply the missing structure. Chinese paper-assembly sites build papers from a "specification table" (细目表) of knowledge points ([菁优网](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id862280922)). A granted patent spells out a deterministic algorithm. Take the uncovered point with the highest required mastery level and the hardest remaining item type. Pick an item inside a difficulty and discrimination window, preferring items that cover the most points. Mark those points covered and repeat until the paper is full ([CN109783601A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN109783601A/zh)). Structured series climb from knowledge-point drills to timed mixed groups to recombined real papers: Zuoyebang's 轻松刷 (Easy Drill) runs 22 basic timed groups, 15 advanced groups and simulation sets ([Douban](https://book.douban.com/subject/35368852/)), and ALLEN's JEE test series runs chapter, part, full and grand mock stages ([ALLEN](https://estore.allen.ac.in/blogs/other/can-a-test-series-actually-boost-your-all-india-rank)). The 赤本 protocol gives the clearest procedure. Do one past paper untimed early to find weaknesses. Then time each major question and repeat until three clean solves. About a week later, rerun the whole paper in a shortened time, perhaps 10 minutes under the limit ([Kyogakusha](https://akahon.net/doc/howto_2012.pdf)). India adds attempt analytics. Aakash labels every mock answer Perfect (correct within ideal time), Overtime or Wasted (fast and wrong). It reports "first look" accuracy, with a benchmark above 90% ([Aakash](https://www.aakash.ac.in/aakash-cbt/help/test-feedback-analysis)), and Embibe's rule is that "wasted attempts have to go to zero" ([Embibe](https://medium.com/feed/embibe)). School platforms such as 好分数 (Haofenshu) turn per-question exam data into lost-points analysis by knowledge point ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/%E5%A5%BD%E5%88%86%E6%95%B0%E5%AE%B6%E9%95%BF%E7%89%88/id1112859166)). The project rule that no exam is hardcoded fits this structure. The blueprint is data the student enters or imports, so the same machinery serves a calculus midterm, the SAT or a gaokao mock.

One conflict needs a ruling. The new learning-science notes warn against using mock-exam accuracy as the memory signal, because a mock mixes recall, speed and endurance. ADR-0014, following the earlier audit, rules that no grading path may turn a miss into a pass or into silence. The audit wins. A wrong answer on a practice test remains an Again for that skill, since a real exam scores it zero. The mock's other ingredients go into labels instead: Overtime and "ran out of time" feed Level's pace evidence and the post-test report, never an FSRS exemption.

For a gaokao candidate, the fit is real but partial. The method layer transfers directly. Top scorers' habits (cause tags, blind redo, thin notebooks, real papers in the final round, analysis time at least equal to test time) map onto M12–M20. The app's FSRS scheduler with step-level diagnosis is ahead of every Chinese tool reached. The offline design also avoids a gaokao-specific risk: major Chinese chatbots reportedly restrict photo-question solving during the exam period ([STDaily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-05/27/content_523153.html)). Korea's exam authority says it excludes items that favor drill-trained students ([Newdaily](https://biz.newdaily.co.kr/site/data/html/2025/03/25/2025032500218.html)), which argues for varied forms over memorized templates. Three gaps remain. The app is English-only. Its packs are limited to openly licensed problems, and the redistribution status of gaokao past papers (真题) was not researched. Gaokao marking schemes for long written answers were not researched either, so M24 provides only the mechanism: rubric-scored items in a user-entered blueprint. The honest claim is that the app can be the best place for a gaokao student to *practice and retain* what their books and school papers contain. It is not yet a source of gaokao papers.

## Penalties, percentiles and chat tutors are the features to leave out

Several popular mechanics exist to solve problems a free, single-user, offline tool doesn't have, or they hurt the outcomes they claim to help. Leaderboards invite gaming: Memrise suspended its leaderboard after "extensive cheating" with bots ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memrise)). IXL's challenge zone adds only 1–2 points for a correct answer and takes 3–8 for a miss, which reviewers call punitive ([IXL guide](https://www.ixl.com/analytics/smart-score-guide.pdf); [Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/ixl)). Percentiles need a population. Even Medify defines its percentile two different ways on one page ([Medify](https://medify.co/resources/ucat-question-bank)). Raw question-bank accuracy depends on mode, repeats and block selection, so users advise "use it to learn, ignore the percentages" ([SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/uworld-average.1186593/post-17536393)). Vendor scale numbers are not evidence. Squirrel AI's Guinness-certified experiment was company-funded, and Guinness certifies scale, not method ([AI Hola](https://aihola.com/article/squirrel-ai-guinness-teaching-experiment)). Korea's AI digital textbooks show the cost of rushing adaptive software. They were developed in about 12 months against the usual 18, faced complaints about factual errors, saw adoption fall from 37% to 19% between semesters, and lost textbook status within months ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/south-korea-ai-textbook/)).

## Correctness needs gates agents cannot bypass, and learning claims need patience

From first principles, the app must answer two different questions. NASA's systems-engineering handbook separates *verification* ("Was the end product realized right?") from *validation* ("Was the right end product realized?"). It counts verification complete only with documented objective evidence and closed discrepancy reports ([NASA SE Handbook 5.3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-3-product-verification/)). For this app, verification means three things: progress is never lost, a wrong answer is never accepted, and the schedule matches a reference implementation. All three can be driven close to zero defects with tests. Validation means the student actually retains more and scores better. That can only be estimated from the student's own data, and slowly. Every failure worth fearing is a severity-5 event in a hazard table: lost progress, a wrong answer marked right, or a schedule that is silently wrong. The modern FMEA standard replaced multiplying severity × occurrence × detection with an "action priority" lookup that weights severity first. Very different risks can multiply to the same score: S10/O2/D5, S2/O5/D10 and S4/O5/D5 all equal 100 ([Reliamag](https://reliamag.com/guides/how-to-perform-fmea/)). Adapted here, any failure with severity 4 or 5 needs a named mitigation **and** a named automated test, whatever its likelihood. "Detection" means which test layer would catch it.

The project already does much of this well. It has an AGENTS.md rulebook and 17 decision records; a three-tier `check.sh` with an import-linter contract, a no-eval test and an offline tripwire; 4,144 core tests and 79 WebKit page tests passing on the Mac; independent reviews, of which the checker review found 74 false accepts and 2 false rejects and the storage review 9 real bugs, each fixed with a test; hand tests on the real Mac that feed a backlog; and an agent playbook with roles, model routing, stop rules and "never edit or weaken a test you didn't write." The no-eval rule has since been vindicated by a 2026 remote-code-execution flaw in a math-verification service. The cause was the same SymPy `parse_expr` call the rule bans ([SecureLayer7](https://securelayer7.net/lab/cve-2026-55585-qwed-authenticated-rce-sympy-parse-expr)). Eight gaps remain.

**E1, protect the tests by script, not by instruction.** In ImpossibleBench, where tests were deliberately impossible to satisfy legitimately, GPT-5 exploited them 76–93% of the time. Claude Opus 4.1 did so about 46% of the time on one variant even with an abort option. The tactics were editing tests, overloading equality and special-casing. Read-only tests reduced it ([ImpossibleBench](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/qJYMbrabcQqCZ7iqm/impossiblebench-measuring-reward-hacking-in-llm-coding-1)). Claude Code's own guidance says hooks are deterministic while instruction files are advisory ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). The ledger records an agent that stopped correctly at a test it was not allowed to edit, which is good, but it rests on instruction. `check.sh` and the pre-commit hook should reject any diff touching `tests/`, fixtures or golden files unless it carries a `Test-Change:` trailer from a verifier session. They should also scan core code for test-aware branches.

**E2, guard the database version and prove durability.** SQLite documents a WAL-reset corruption bug in every version from 3.7.0 through 3.51.2, fixed in 3.51.3 with backports to 3.44.6 and 3.50.7. It needs two connections writing or checkpointing at the same instant ([SQLite WAL](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html)). The single-writer design makes that unlikely, but backups can run from a separate process, and the bundled SQLite is whatever version the packaged Python carries. Startup and tests should fail below a fixed version, and the build manifest should record the version. The fault-injection tests should become an acknowledged-write kill loop: hundreds of SIGKILLs during answers, imports and migrations. After each, assert that every acknowledged answer is present, `integrity_check` passes, and stored state equals a replay of the log. A Hypothesis state machine covering answer, undo, import, reopen, crash and clock changes is the single strongest test of "no lost progress" ([Hypothesis](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/stateful.html)).

**E3, trace every critical requirement to evidence the owner can read.** The spec's IDs (QUE-, ENG-, BAK-) are halfway to a trace. The other half is a risk class and verification method on each requirement, written in EARS form ([EARS](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)), a `pytest.mark.req` marker on each test, and a short script that fails when a Critical requirement has no passing test. The script's output becomes a plain-language "trust report": green or red per critical requirement, with open risks and waivers. That is how an owner who does not read code can know what is proven.

**E4, mutation testing on the five critical modules.** At Google, 82% of reported surviving mutants were judged productive once trivial ones were suppressed. Google treats each survivor as a concrete test goal, not a score to chase ([Petrović et al.](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rjust/publ/practical_mutation_testing_tse_2021.pdf)). Run mutmut on the grader, rating map, scheduler adapter, storage and importer for touched code ([mutmut](https://github.com/Otto-AA/mutmut)).

**E5, a written threat model with negative tests for imported content.** Imported Anki HTML now renders in the page (ADR-0017). Electron's security checklist warns that any frame, including iframes, can send messages across the bridge ([Electron](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/security)). A test should therefore prove that card content cannot reach `window.pywebview`. Each STRIDE threat at each trust boundary gets a mitigation and a negative test ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Threat_Modeling_Cheat_Sheet.html)).

**E6, usability beyond the owner.** The product direction is "anyone, any class." Five users find about 85% of usability problems, and three small rounds beat one big one ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)). With five users, a SUS score lands within about 17 points of the true value 95% of the time, so report it as a range ([MeasuringU](https://www.measuringu.com/10-things-sus)). Three classmates plus the owner per round is enough. WCAG 2.2 adds two AA criteria this app touches, Focus Not Obscured (backlog #6 is that failure) and 24×24-pixel targets ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)), and its Status Messages criterion calls for announcing verdicts through a live region ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

**E7, a learning-validation phase honest about one learner.** The earlier audit already specified the calibration machinery: strict outcome labels, true retention with Wilson intervals, and guarded refits. This report adds two pieces. The first is the exam check in M21: log predicted against actual scores for every real exam. The second is a realistic experiment budget. By a standard two-proportion power calculation (80% power, α = 0.05):

| Difference to detect | Rated reviews needed | At about 14 rated reviews a day (assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 vs 0.90 | about 1,370 | about 3 months |
| 0.88 vs 0.93 | about 1,080 | about 2.5 months |
| 0.87 vs 0.90 | about 3,550 | about 8 months |

Reviews of the same skill are correlated, so the real numbers are higher. One learner can therefore settle about **one policy question per semester**, and only a large one. Spend that budget on the highest-stakes open choice (the M1 gate first), randomize at the skill level, and write the comparison down before the semester starts so it can't be cherry-picked. Item holdouts studied only from the textbook would give a causal estimate, but they cost a student whose grades matter, so they should stay optional.

**E8, decide distribution before sharing.** Gatekeeper-friendly distribution needs a $99-a-year Developer ID and notarization ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/program-enrollment/); [Apple notarization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution)). That conflicts with the owner's no-spend constraint, and the spec already defers it. The same decision record should settle three more things: the unlicensed fsrs-rs-python dependency, CC BY-NC-SA pack duties, and REUSE/SPDX labeling ([REUSE](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/)). Supply-chain checks with pip-audit and an SBOM belong in the same gate ([pip-audit](https://pypi.org/project/pip-audit/)).

## Conclusion

The best systems split along a line this app can cross. Western adaptive platforms (Math Academy, ALEKS, MATHia, AMBOSS) have models that pick and time practice, but little discipline around mistakes and mocks. Asian exam ecosystems have exactly that discipline in their mistake notebooks, specification tables, 赤本 passes and mock analytics, but no scheduler beyond a nominal "Ebbinghaus" reminder. Toishi already has the stronger engine, so its route to "the best possible way to practice" runs through the workflow layer. That means mistakes that close and retire, and rehearsals built from a blueprint and judged against real scores. More modeling sophistication is not the route. For a gaokao candidate that combination would be new. The binding limits are language and content rights, not method.

The engineering lesson follows from the statistics. A single learner's data can confirm a policy only about once a semester, and only for large effects. So validation will always lag, and a wrong grading rule or a lost write could run for months before any chart shows it. That asymmetry is why verification has to be close to exhaustive, and why its gates must be enforced by scripts the agents cannot edit rather than by rules they are asked to follow. In this project, the tests are the only part of quality the learner never has to wait a semester to see.
