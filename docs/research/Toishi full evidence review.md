# Fix the Seams, Then Ship Calculus I

Yes, the spec makes sense, and the built core matches it. Toishi rests on principles with real evidence behind them: spaced practice of a problem type, a worked example before practice, a full explanation after every answer, unreadable input treated as invalid rather than wrong, and numeric-first answer checking. Its data core is the strongest part of the build: durable storage, a grader with **2,726 golden cases**, an isolated FSRS adapter and a rating map that matches the spec row for row. But "backed by research" is true of the principles, not the numbers. Most of the spec's more than one hundred constants are design guesses. The central bet, FSRS-6 scheduling *skills* with a fresh problem at each review, has never been tested anywhere. And a few rules are contradicted by the project's own honest-grading principle or by the researchers' own simulations. The correctness defects cluster where a second path reaches an outcome without a guard the main path has. Easy below-band problems write rated reviews that train the fitter. Practice-test misses at exam tiers 4 and 5 go unrated. Undo after a shown solution appears, from a code reading not yet reproduced, to permit a clean Good. Fitted parameters are adopted in three separate writes. The partial-credit switch would approve useless credit about half the time, and the placement check seeds only about a tenth of what a half-knowing student knows (both of the last two are the researchers' own calculations). The spec lags its decision records (ADRs) on five Critical rules, says in one place that 1.0 ships no course pack, and plans a release candidate (RC) around November 23, although no Calculus I card exists and the M7 verification work was stopped minutes after it started. Compared with other apps, the gaps that matter are faithful flashcards for imported Anki decks, a practice-only mode, in-app restore, printed papers (which the owner's paper mock exams now cover) and reach: a Mac-only app sees roughly one in eight US web page views, about one in 37 in China and one in 77 in India. The owner's no-AI decision is the best-supported choice in the whole design. The measured harms of AI tutors come from letting AI do the student's work, and the measured benefits come from structure this app already has. The plan below puts the algorithm and durability fixes, the M7 gates, restore and bridge hardening before onboarding. Content waits for a pilot section whose cost and error rate have been measured.

## The design holds, but its numbers and seams are unproven

The spec is coherent and, in its principles, better evidenced than most commercial practice software. Its strongest decisions are structural. Shipped content and the student's progress live in separate files linked by permanent IDs. Grading follows ADR-0014's rule that "no rule may turn a miss into a pass, an unseen problem into a miss, or a reminder-aided answer into a measurement" ([ADR-0014](/docs/decisions/0014-honest-grading-and-queue-parity.md)). Fitted parameters are adopted only when they win on held-out data, and memory is rebuilt from the review log on one whole-study-day clock ([ADR-0013](/docs/decisions/0013-fsrs-fitting-clock-and-memory.md)). The checker compares numerically first, and its symbolic step can confirm a match but never overrule a mismatch ([spec](/docs/spec.md)). Nothing the student types is evaluated as code. CVE-2026-55585 vindicates that rule: a high-severity (CVSS 8.8) remote-code-execution flaw caused by exactly the SymPy `parse_expr` call the project bans ([OSV](https://api.osv.dev/v1/vulns/CVE-2026-55585)). The design is completed by offline operation by construction, durability-first storage, and a plain-language trust report for an owner who does not read code. All of it is worth protecting.

The research behind the principles is solid. Spacing practice of a mathematics problem type is worth **g = 0.28** across 27 studies ([Murray et al. 2025](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/229807)). Worked examples are worth about **g = 0.48** ([Barbieri et al.](https://oaks.kent.edu/hcri/meta-analysis-worked-examples-effect-mathematics-performance)). Explanatory feedback beats right-or-wrong feedback, **d = 0.49 versus 0.05** ([van der Kleij et al. 2015](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3102/0034654314564881)). Rejecting unreadable input as invalid rather than wrong is the settled practice of STACK, the main open-source maths assessment engine ([STACK](https://docs.stack-assessment.org/en/Authoring/Inputs)).

The numbers are another matter. The spec review counted well over a hundred numeric constants. The spec cites a source for very few, and earlier reports source roughly a third. The rest are starting guesses: the timing model, the slow-answer rule, the slip cap, the blame weights, the band thresholds, the course-sharing weights and the leech threshold ([spec](/docs/spec.md); spec review). The central bet is also untested. FSRS-6's benchmark comes from Anki flashcard reviews ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)), and no study tests FSRS on skills served with fresh problems, which the spec admits. With default parameters at 0.90 retention, a skill answered Good every time gets intervals of **2, 11, 46, 163 and 365 days** (researchers' own calculation, [engine notes](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/engine_decisions_evidence.md)). That is far more aggressive than ASSISTments' 7, 14, 28 and 56-day reassessment ladder ([ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org/blog-posts/how-assistments-skillbuilders-works-in-my-class-and-why-it-will-work-for-you-too)), which has a randomized trial behind it. The 46- and 163-day steps are therefore extrapolations that only the app's own calibration log can confirm.

The build matches the spec where it matters most. A sample of 41 requirement rows found **30 implemented as written with a named test**, 8 with a deviation or a missing piece, and 3 not built: restore inside the app, the SQLite version guard, and DEV-9 to DEV-11 (the stateful property test, the requirement trace and the trust report). The suite collects **4,409 tests** without mocks, including parity checks against Anki 26.9.3 and fsrs-rs-python ([built-system review](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/built_system_review.md)).

Two things are off. First, effort went into data-hungry engine features first: shadow partial credit, the weak-spot finder, calibration statistics, automatic fitting and per-domain parameter sets. These pay off only after weeks of data, while onboarding, goals, exams, the import screen, restore, function plots and every Calculus I card remain unbuilt. They should be frozen, not removed. Second, critical code is concentrated. The study service is one 2,862-line class with 84 methods. The one duplicated-logic bug found, a same-day freshness rule added to the problem picker but not to its copy in the check picker, is exactly what that concentration breeds.

Expectations should come from what the best tools achieve. ASSISTments, the strongest homework-practice evidence, gained **+0.18 SD** in Maine and **+0.10 SD** in a 63-school replication ([Evidence for ESSA](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=630)). A two-year randomized trial of Khan Academy with its Khanmigo AI tutor found **0.039 SD** pooled. Its authors credit "structured, individually targeted practice rather than conversational tutoring" ([Oreopoulos & Low](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)). A whole practice package moves outcomes by about a tenth to a third of a standard deviation, which is why honest measurement and the student's adherence matter more here than further modeling.

## Twenty defects, mostly where a second path skips a guard

The defects below are ranked by what they damage. **Class A** silently corrupts the memory model, Level or saved progress: the spec's hazard classes for lost progress, a wrong answer marked right, and a schedule that is silently wrong (HAZ-1 to HAZ-3, severity 5). **Class B** is wrong behavior the student can see, or a weakened guarantee. The basis column says how each was established. "Code" means the path was read end to end, in this review or the built-system review. "Code (not run)" means a strong code reading that was not reproduced at run time. "Sim", "probe" and "experiment" mark the researchers' own simulations, grader runs and bridge experiments, not literature. Most share one pattern: a second route into an outcome that skips a guard the main route has. That is exactly what the unbuilt state-machine test (DEV-9) exists to catch.

Three statistical rules misfire without being bugs, and they belong in the same work queue. The weak-spot finder's z ≤ −2 screen nominally flags 2.3% of normal skills. Its real per-evaluation rate swings between 0.9% and 7.4% with sample size. Its fixed three-to-four-problem confirmation set confirms a real gap (true success 0.6 against 0.8 expected) only 35–53% of the time. A sequential test with at most eight problems confirms that gap 67% of the time at about the same false-confirmation rate (researchers' calculation, [sim_sprt.py](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/scripts/sim_sprt.py)). QUE-4's rule that no skill waits more than 7 days turns the "highest recall first" default into near "oldest due first" under any lasting backlog, within 0.1 point of mean recall (researchers' simulation, [sim_triage2.py](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/scripts/sim_triage2.py)). That is fine, but the spec should say so. And the first-seen-versus-repeated-form alarm in ENG-5 has about a 7% false-alarm rate and only 49% power for a real 15-point gap at 30 pairs (researchers' calculation, [stats2.py](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/scripts/stats2.py)). It is a canary, not a test.

Several rating rules are not defects but untested hypotheses that write FSRS grades: Hard for a slow correct answer, a second attempt, a non-final hint or an "unsure" submission. If slowness carries no forgetting signal, grading 10% of passes Hard instead of Good raises workload by about **19%** with no gain in recall (researchers' simulation, [sim_hardshare.py](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/scripts/sim_hardshare.py)). Response time adds only **+0.0008 to +0.0123** R² to a knowledge-tracing model ([EDM 2023](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2023/proceedings/2023.EDM-short-papers.22/index.html)). Anki's FSRS FAQ says using only Again and Good "may even be more accurate" ([FSRS FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/frequently-asked-questions-about-fsrs)). Keep the both-conditions slow rule, but schedule its test: once there are about 300 long-term items, compare held-out log loss with and without each Hard path, scored against the strict outcome label the app already logs.

## Principles are well evidenced; most constants have none

The verdicts use five labels. **Supported** means direct evidence (meta-analyses, randomized trials or binding standards) for the principle as built. **Weakly supported** means indirect, small, vendor or principle-only evidence, with unsourced numbers. **Unsupported** means no evidence either way: a design guess. **Contradicted** means evidence, an applicable standard, the project's own adopted principle or the researchers' own arithmetic points against the claim or rule. **Missing** means the evidence calls for something the spec or build lacks. Vendor claims are marked as such.

## Anki parity, printed papers and reach are what others have

The four families of competitors teach different lessons. **Anki and the spaced-repetition apps** show that Toishi already beats them on exams, backlogs, typed-math grading, hints and diagnosis, but that its flashcard half "works like Anki" and inherits Anki's known problems. On Anki's forum, the second most-liked suggestion asks how to stop students misusing the Hard button (352 likes), and a request to make Pass/Fail the default drew 167 ([thread 49092](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/49092); [thread 34147](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/34147)). Imported decks will also bring nested clozes (supported in Anki since [25.07](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/releases/tag/25.07)), `[latex]` markup and reversed card pairs that this importer does not yet handle ([ADR-0015](/docs/decisions/0015-anki-import.md)). **Asian exam tools** show the bookkeeping half of the mistake loop: capture, three same-type items, print, correct, retest. Every vendor surveyed ships print or PDF. Korea's EBSi builds a test from only a student's weak parts ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/id669006959)). Gaokao keys mark long answers step by step with cumulative marks, and multi-select items score 5, 2 or 0 ([泰安 mock key](https://mjy.js.org/resources/%E8%BF%91%E6%9C%9F%E5%85%A8%E5%9B%BD%E5%90%84%E5%9C%B0%E9%AB%98%E8%80%83%E6%A8%A1%E6%8B%9F%E5%8D%B7/%E3%80%90%E6%96%B0%E9%AB%98%E8%80%83%E3%80%91%E5%B1%B1%E4%B8%9C%E6%B3%B0%E5%AE%89%E9%AB%98%E4%B8%899%E6%9C%88%E9%98%B6%E6%AE%B5%E6%B5%8B%E8%AF%95%EF%BC%889.30%E6%9B%B4%E6%96%B0%EF%BC%8C%E9%AB%98%E6%B8%85%EF%BC%89/%E6%95%B0%E5%AD%A6%E7%AD%94%E6%A1%88.pdf)). None of these tools runs a parameterized scheduler, so the method layer is where this app can lead. **Homework systems** such as PrairieLearn, WebAssign and Mastering show the attempt policy and the answer types a general STEM app will need. PrairieLearn separates parsing from grading so format errors cost nothing ([PrairieLearn docs](https://docs.prairielearn.com/question/server/)). Physics tolerances run 1 to 3%, not 0.1%. Hiding intermediate values makes errors cascade through multi-part problems ([Sydney MasteringPhysics report](https://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/~obyrne/MPreport_v2.pdf)). Proof Blocks matched written proofs on the post-test in about a quarter of the time (11.1 versus 43.4 minutes; [Poulsen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09609)). **AI tutors** offer features that can be copied without AI. In Bastani's trial, AI-assisted practice scores rose 48% while exam scores fell 17%, so success with outside help is performance, not learning ([Bastani et al.](https://nmdprojects.net/teaching_resources/bastani_generative_ai_can_harm_learning.pdf)). Students in an econometrics course who got guidance on *how* to use AI scored 0.218 SD higher on the final, while encouragement to adopt it did nothing ([IZA DP 18513](https://docs.iza.org/dp18513.pdf)).

Several features stay out on purpose. **All runtime AI** stays out under decision 1: chat, bring-your-own-key helpers, generated explanations, photo reading by cloud or local models (which drops the backlog's math-OCR spike), and a "copy a grounded prompt to your own AI" button. That button keeps AI out of the app's code but builds the AI-tutor workflow the owner ruled out. The **guided redo** waits until data shows the same misses repeating (decision 3). **Sync, accounts, add-ons and custom scheduling code** stay out because they break the offline and no-eval rules. **Presets and next-interval button labels** stay out because they are a known source of Anki confusion ([thread 58773](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/58773); [inventory](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/anki_and_srs_feature_inventory.md)). **XP, leagues, leaderboards and PK battles** stay out: Memrise suspended its leaderboard after "extensive cheating" with bots ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memrise)), and external rewards are unrelated to persistence ([Howard et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620966789)). **Percentiles** are meaningless without a norm group. **Photo answer search and barcode answer lookup** are cloud-bound and answer-giving. **Parent dashboards** need accounts. **Image occlusion** waits until a pack needs diagram labeling. **Anki's hourly, ease and answer-button charts** add nothing for problems. **Running student code inside the web view** stays out: seven products that sandboxed Python in Pyodide were escaped in 2026 ([Cyera](https://www.cyera.com/research/sandcastles-not-sandboxes-how-one-architectural-flaw-exposed-seven-products)).

## Reach, licensing and money are the owner's open choices

The research cannot make the following decisions; it can only price them. The project's stated constraints frame each one: no spending beyond the Claude plan, no GitHub, building and testing on a Mac, English-only for now, open-source problems only, and a bar of "good enough that someone prepping for the gaokao could use it and come out on top".

**Reach is the largest gap between the goal and the build.** By page-view share, a Mac-only app reaches about 12.8% of US web traffic, 2.7% of China's and 1.3% of India's (researchers' arithmetic from StatCounter: [US desktop OS](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/desktop/united-states-of-america), [China desktop OS](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/desktop/china), [India platform](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/india)). Chinese minors use phones (91.3%) and tablets (45.5%) far more than desktops (33.2%) ([CNNIC](https://www.cnnic.cn/NMediaFile/2023/1225/MAIN1703484375296SPBHV29S0V.pdf)). On Chinese desktops, Windows has 92% of page views. School-managed Chromebooks that block the Linux environment rule out desktop installs ([reach notes](file:///home/claude/research_notes/Toishi%20full%20evidence%20review/reach_platform_community.md)). A gaokao candidate, one of **12.9 million** in 2026 ([Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20260603/8c64a5880000444d9f3ce86864b064e1/c.html)), is therefore unlikely to own an Apple-silicon Mac.

The cheapest broad-reach path is a web build that runs the existing pure-Python core in Pyodide. Python, the standard library, SymPy and mpmath come to about **17 MB** raw (researchers' measurement from the [Pyodide CDN](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/pyodide/v314.0.7/full/)). It works only because the spec already keeps the core free of any interface code. But it reverses three rules (the app "never opens a network connection", the loopback server, and no browser storage), and Safari deletes a site's storage after 7 days without use unless the app is installed to the home screen ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)). It therefore needs its own ADR and a go/no-go spike that runs the grader golden suite and scheduler vectors inside Pyodide. Windows comes second. Microsoft Store registration is free for individuals, and Microsoft re-signs MSIX packages at no cost ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/partner-center/open-a-developer-account)). An iPad version is a re-architecture, not a port, because Python on iOS cannot start the separate process the grader uses to kill runaway algebra ([Python docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/intro.html)).

**Licensing has three separate questions.** First, fsrs-rs-python has no licence, so it cannot be redistributed inside an app. The options, cheapest first: ask its maintainers to add BSD-3 to match the underlying crate, write a thin binding over the BSD-3 `fsrs` crate, or ship without fitting. Second, the app's own code has no licence. An OSI-approved licence is the entry ticket to free code signing through SignPath ([terms](https://signpath.org/terms)) and free translation hosting on Crowdin ([Crowdin](https://crowdin.com/page/open-source-project-setup-request)), and Creative Commons advises against its licences for software ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). Third, CC BY-NC-SA for packs fits the NC sources (OpenStax moved to BY-NC-SA, [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/license)) but is not "open" by the Open Definition ([Open Definition](https://opendefinition.org/od/2.1/en/)). It blocks adapting BY-SA sources, and it may license AI-written problems that carry no copyright. Original content could go out under CC BY or CC0, with NC kept only where inherited. For gaokao use, real past papers stay out: a Beijing case upheld copyright in GMAT test questions ([GMAC](https://www.gmac.com/why-gmac/gmac-news/gmac-press-releases/beijing-court-upholds-copyright-protection-for-gmat-exam)).

**Money and continuity.** Apple's Developer Program fee waiver excludes individuals and sole proprietors ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/membership-fee-waiver/)), so Mac distribution to others means $99 a year, an "Open Anyway" step, or a build-from-source script. The largest volunteer app in this space, AnkiDroid, runs on an estimated **$71,793** a year from 8,552 contributors ([Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/ankidroid)). A new project should expect far less and keep its running costs near zero. About 65% of popular open-source projects would be crippled by losing two developers ([Avelino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06766)), and Anki itself moved stewardship to AnkiHub in 2026 so the ecosystem "will no longer be in jeopardy if I'm one day hit by a bus" ([Anki forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610.md)). NLnet grants of €5,000 to €50,000 require a European dimension and that "proposals and projects should be made by humans, not AI" ([NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/propose/)), which an agent-built project would struggle to meet.

## Correctness and gates first, content after a measured pilot

The plan follows the owner's decisions: no AI, algorithm correctness first, a great base app before any mass-produced problem sets, and Calculus I first. Agent throughput is not the constraint: M0 to M6 and 18 ADRs were all logged on one day ([agent ledger](/docs/agent-ledger.md)). The binding limits are the owner's review time, verified content, and a release-candidate window that overlaps final-exam preparation. So the release candidate should be gated on the base app's criteria, with the spec's own rule that 1.0 "ships when every criterion below passes, not on a date". The Chapter 4 pack should ship as its own pack release once it passes the content checklist. Two weeks of daily use during final-exam preparation is exactly the use the release candidate is meant to survive.

## Conclusion

In Anki the student's button press is the measurement and the optimizer only listens. In Toishi the app's grading rules write every rating, so each path into a rating is part of the measuring instrument. That reframes the defects above. They are not ordinary bugs but places where the instrument bends: an easy problem counted as recall, an undo that hides a shown solution, a practice-test miss that vanishes, an excusal that never runs out. Because one learner's data can settle only about one policy question per semester, a bent instrument can run for months before any chart reveals it. Exhaustive verification at the seams, enforced by scripts the agents cannot edit, is therefore worth more than any new mechanic, and the owner's choice to put algorithm correctness first is the right reading of that asymmetry.

The competitive picture points the same way. No tool examined combines a parameterized scheduler with the mistake and mock-exam discipline of Asian exam preparation. The evidence on AI tutors says the gains come from verified content, attempt-first flow and help triggered by mistakes, which Toishi builds deterministically and without the substitution harm. The app's path to "the best practice system available" therefore runs through honesty of measurement and the workflow around mistakes and rehearsal, not through more modeling or a chatbot. The real ceiling on "free for everyone" is not method but reach, licences and content rights: a Mac-only build serves the owner, and almost nobody preparing for the gaokao.
