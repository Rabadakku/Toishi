# How top students prepare for high-stakes exams (gaokao, suneung, Japan, JEE) and the tools they use — notes for Toishi, as of 2026-11-01

Evidence tags used below: [A] = anecdote or self-report by a named student or teacher; [V] = vendor or publisher claim; [F] = folk wisdom or opinion piece; [E] = empirical or peer-reviewed research. Non-English terms are translated, with the original in parentheses. Search tooling note: Zhihu (知乎) and Baidu Baike pages were blocked to the fetch tool, so Zhihu-based claims below come from news and Q&A sites instead. Several fetched pages were short or promotional; where a fetch did not support something, it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## 1. Gaokao preparation structure and what top scorers (状元) say they did

### Takeaway
Across the sources I could retrieve, the shared pattern is: fewer, higher-quality problems; mistake records that capture the cause and the reasoning rather than a copy of the question; blind redoing of mistakes on a schedule; real past papers (真题) in the final round; and mocks used as diagnostics, not verdicts. Students disagree on whether a full 错题本 (mistake notebook) is worth its upkeep. None of this is backed by controlled gaokao studies in what I found; it is almost all [A] or [F].

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 gaokao-year student (李佳颖, Wuhan No. 2 Middle School, total 694: 语文129/数学142/英语145/物理92/化学94/生物92) describes a four-step 错题本 method [A]: (1) classify at entry (only questions where thinking got stuck are copied in; careless slips get a one-sentence summary such as "misread a sign" on the first page, skimmed before exams); (2) correct while thinking, answer-peeking allowed; (3) mark mistakes with a triangle, batch them for a week, then redo with the answer covered; (4) once a week, redo the mistakes in an evening self-study session (question in black ink, solution in blue, solution covered during redo). Her head teacher says she "can grind one mistake several times". She treats mock exams (模拟考) as a tool for finding weak sections, not a judgment of her. — [Tencent News, 2026-08-21](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260821A0A5UZ00)
- A 2021 compilation of eight Peking/Tsinghua admits from the book 《等你在清华/北大》 (self-reported scores, years mostly not stated) [A]: do not "do every problem indiscriminately"; pick a few representative problems per knowledge point and skip what you've mastered (熊韦, 654); keep the 错题本 "as thin as possible" at stage reviews by consolidating old entries, and return to the textbook example problems after a mistake (杨春雪, 653); do large volumes but chase quality, from teachers, famous schools' 真题 and 模拟题, and avoid the notebook becoming an empty formality (姜彦文, 682); record the knowledge point behind each error rather than the question (江晓萌, 705 entries); the notebook cost more time than it returned, so skip it if upkeep exceeds benefit, and put time limits on uncertain problems to simulate the exam (曾竞, 638); "few but excellent" practice, deriving many variants from one 母题 (parent problem) (张楚); write reflections in red ink beside each mistake (朱媛韬, 662). The students disagree with each other on notebook thickness and value. — [The Paper (澎湃号), 2021-12-30](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16072260)
- A Hubei special-grade geography teacher (李艳) on the three review rounds (三轮复习) [A]: textbook read three times (concepts; knowledge network/mind map guided by the curriculum standard; checking against real exam questions); the 错题本 should compare errors across mocks and attribute each to concept recall, terminology, logical reasoning, test-taking technique, reading the material, extracting information, or misreading the question; do not buy 押题卷 (predicted-question papers) or do only a few mocks, and make good use of past gaokao papers; in the third round, use real exam questions repeatedly "to build question sense and automate reasoning steps." — [Ifeng / Jiguang News, 2024-04-12](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8YjCawXIS4W)
- A contributed 2026-09-16 article for 初三 (ninth-grade) students [F]: students treat "correcting" as copying the answer; "四改试卷" (four ways to correct a paper): (a) completely unknown, so note the gap and do 1-2 similar problems; (b) guessed or uncertain, so review the reasoning; (c) muddled thinking, so rewrite the steps; (d) inefficient method, so note a faster method and when to use it. "三阶段复盘" (three-stage review): same day, weekly grouped by knowledge module, monthly redo of frequently missed items with "需强化" (needs reinforcement) if wrong again. Claims results in about two weeks, with no data. Not gaokao-specific. — [Beijing News](https://www.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1789525286189470.html)
- An unsigned opinion piece on 错题本 [F]: a notebook becomes a "后悔本" (regret notebook) if never reviewed; each entry should close a loop (mistake, cause, correction, one similar practice problem, one reminder sentence); copying neatly is "moving the error from the test paper to another notebook"; weekly, choose five mistakes, not fifty. — [NetEase 艺文志](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L45VFT0V0556FBHS.html)
- A student write-up on notebook upkeep cost [A]: hand-copying a single math problem took over ten minutes and cost over six hours a week; a teacher suggested doing more practice problems instead; an unsorted notebook took about an hour to find five related questions; a photo-based app that strips handwriting and tags subject, unit, source exam and difficulty fixed this, and it could assemble a practice paper from selected weak questions to retake under timed conditions. — [Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/read/cv50578712)
- Teacher/curriculum framing: the geography teacher cites the 高考评价体系 (gaokao evaluation system) and the 2017 curriculum standard (2020 revision) as the basis for exam questions. — [Ifeng](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8YjCawXIS4W)

### Inferences
- The most consistent "state-of-the-art" practices across independent speakers are: (1) tag the cause at entry, (2) cheap entries for slips and rich entries for real misunderstandings, (3) blind redo of the same skill, (4) retire entries so the notebook stays thin, (5) in the last round, shift from topic drills to timed whole papers.
- The "题海战术" (massive drilling) vs targeted practice debate is mostly resolved in these sources toward targeted practice, but one scorer (姜彦文) says volume is still necessary. A spaced scheduler that delivers fresh variants of weak skills is arguably the formalization of "targeted volume."
- The sources are self-reported by high scorers (survivorship bias); nobody here compared a notebook-using group to a non-notebook group.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve 状元 interviews from the 2024-2026 gaokao years beyond the single 694-point student; Zhihu answers were blocked to the fetcher.
- No quantitative data on how many past-paper years or how many mocks top scorers do. The geography teacher's advice is qualitative.
- Time-allocation data for the three rounds (when 一轮 ends, how long 二轮 lasts) was not found in a fetchable source. (Round structure per practice-book usage is in Section 2.)
- Pacing drills (限时训练) are only mentioned in passing (曾竞; 必刷题 usage in Section 2); no systematic source on 考前 (pre-exam) routines was found.

## 2. Tools gaokao students actually use (practice books, school platforms, learning devices, mistake printers, apps)

### Takeaway
The gaokao tool ecosystem has four layers: print practice-book series organized by chapter/topic/round/mock paper; school-run scoring platforms that give score and knowledge-point diagnostics; consumer devices and apps (learning machines, photo-search apps, mistake printers) that capture wrong questions and push similar ones; and paper-based 错题本 habits. The sources I could verify are thin on the specific named book series and on 七天网络/极课大数据; the best-evidenced items are the 必刷题 usage pattern, iFlytek's and 猿辅导's feature claims, and student-reported notebook costs.

### Cited Findings

**2.1 Practice-book series (教辅)**
- 《高中必刷题》 (one author's account, not the publisher's) [F]: structured per chapter with a "基础篇" (basic) and "提升篇" (advanced) section, plus "专题板块" (topic modules such as 导数综合题 derivative problems, 圆锥曲线定值定点 conic fixed-point problems, 立体几何外接球 circumscribed spheres), plus mock paper sets and 真题 compilations. Suggested round use: 一轮 (consolidation): go chapter by chapter, basic then advanced, understanding over speed; 二轮 (topic breakthrough): drill topic modules and build a "question type to method" mapping; 三轮 (sprint/mock): timed mock sets or adapted 真题 for time allocation, sort mistakes into knowledge gap / wrong method / calculation slip; rule of thumb "spend 10-15 minutes reviewing after each set." The "basic and medium questions are the main scoring zone" ratio is the author's opinion. — [Sina](https://sina.cn/news/detail/5277040501592120.html)
- Junior-high (not gaokao) guide, June 2023 [F]: classifies study aids into 同步讲解册, 同步练习册, 同步单元卷, 专项能力提升辅导书, and 错题本; calls 《五三》 the easiest base option and other brands (including 必刷题) harder; warns against stacking too many practice books. **2.6 What top scorers and teachers say is worth using vs not**
- Worth using (mostly [A]): your own mistakes with cause tags and a reminder note; past real exam papers and well-chosen school 模拟题; textbook example problems after a mistake; a small number of high-quality problems per knowledge point; self-made mind maps (the 694 scorer says copying an online mind map is not enough). — [Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260821A0A5UZ00); [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16072260)
- Wastes time (mostly [A]/[F]): hand-copying whole questions; unsorted or untagged notebooks; indiscriminate drilling; 押题卷; stacking too many 教辅; notebooks that are never re-tested. — [Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/read/cv50578712); [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16072260); [Ifeng](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8YjCawXIS4W); [NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L45VFT0V0556FBHS.html)

### Inferences
- Feature pattern across 好分数/智学网/devices: a loop of exam data to weak knowledge points to personalized plan, closed by "测-学-练" (test, learn, practice). Toishi already has the equivalent (placement test, skill graph, scheduled practice); the gap is exam-data ingestion (mocks scored per skill).
- Vendor "billions of questions" and "1.12 billion knowledge points" are marketing scale numbers [V]; they are not evidence of better learning. Chinese consumers reportedly buy on content (66.92%), which is the one dimension a free open-source problem bank can compete on.
- The photo-to-mistake pipeline's value in sources is time saved on copying; the learning value comes from tagging and re-testing, which hardware does not supply by itself.

## 3. Korea (suneung), Japan (university entrance exams), and India (JEE)

### Takeaway
All three have the same core loop as the gaokao: error log with cause labels, past papers (赤本 / 기출 / PYQs), timed mocks, and analysis time that rivals test time. Korea adds an explicit policy angle: the exam body tries to exclude items that favor private-drilling training and links about half of items indirectly to EBS materials. Japan has the most concretely specified past-paper protocol (publisher guide). JEE evidence is mostly topper interviews and low-quality listicles, some of which are unreliable.

### Cited Findings

**Korea (수능 / suneung, 오답노트 = wrong-answer note)**
- A perfect-math-score student (조아영, Korea University sophomore, grade 1 in every subject, math had been grade 7 in her first year of high school) [A]: math wrong problems recorded separately, sorted by importance and by type; each entry gets a margin label for error cause (fully understood / simple calculation mistake / didn't know the concept / needed to apply a known concept to a new problem); study reminders beside entries; notebook reviewed as often as possible, skimmed whole and the most-missed key points drilled just before the exam, and brought into the exam hall; make sure you understand the concept before writing the entry; no notebook for Korean (국어), instead analyze every mock-exam problem for why a trap choice was chosen; for students who never kept a notebook, start with the June and September 평가원 (KICE) mock exams and track only those wrong answers; in the final month review what you know rather than learning new material. — [Donghwa News](https://www.dhnews.co.kr/news/view/179512497334451)
- Exam design (2026학년도 수능 basic plan, announced 2025-03-25; exam date 2025-11-13): difficulty set so that completing public-school curriculum supplemented with EBS materials suffices; items favoring private-education problem-drilling are excluded; EBS linkage is indirect, about 50% of items per subject, using the linked books' tables, figures and passages; two official mocks in June and September; math 30 items, Korean 45, English 45 (listening 17 within 25 minutes); English and Korean History graded on absolute scale; Korean History non-attendance voids the whole exam. — [Newdaily](https://biz.newdaily.co.kr/site/data/html/2025/03/25/2025032500218.html)

**Japan (大学入試; 過去問 = past papers; 赤本 = Kyogakusha's red-cover past-paper books)**
- Publisher guide (教学社, 2011/2012 editions) [V, primary]: get the 赤本 early and do one year once before finishing the syllabus to find weaknesses; average 5.3 赤本 volumes used by 100 successful candidates (2010 survey); first pass: one full year, untimed, to understand the whole and plan an approach; read the explanations carefully; from the second pass, time each 大問 (major question), mark ○ for questions solved smoothly, and repeat until there are three ○; about a week later redo the full set in a shortened time; setting the timer 10 minutes shorter than the real exam is one option; log mistakes and the reasons in a "赤本ノート" (red-book notebook) for quick re-reading; read the "傾向と対策" (trends and countermeasures) section and annotate it; also solve past papers of similar universities or other faculties for practice. — [Kyogakusha 赤本 guide (PDF)](https://akahon.net/doc/howto_2012.pdf)
- A Toyo Keizai article warns that examinees often neglect review as the exam nears, and that reviewing only the wrong answers can backfire; it advises writing down why an answer was wrong (subheading only; body text was not in the fetched page). — [Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/848263?display=b)

**India (JEE Main/Advanced; coaching: Allen, FIITJEE, Physics Wallah)**
- JEE Advanced 2025 toppers (Rajit Gupta, AIR 1, 322/360; Sakshyam Jindal, Common Rank List 2) both attended Allen; Jindal: "Regular analysis of your mock test will improve your concept" and helps with exam-day panic; Gupta mainly used NCERT with selected HC Verma and Irodov questions, no fixed daily hours, and the article does not attribute a mock strategy to him. — [The Week / PTI, 2025-06-02](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/06/02/del18-rj-jee-advanced-toppers.html)
- JEE Advanced 2026 final-45-day guide (CollegeDekho) [F for the cadence]: toppers spend more time analyzing mocks than taking them; review wrong answers, skipped questions you could have solved, and time-consuming topics; many keep an error notebook. Vavilala Chidvilas Reddy (AIR 1, JEE Advanced 2023) [A] spent 2-3 hours analyzing each paper, asking whether each error was a conceptual gap, calculation error, or silly mistake. The suggested mock cadence (2-3 per week, then one every 2-3 days, then 3-4 selected in the last 15 days) and "most toppers" claims are unattributed. — [CollegeDekho](https://www.collegedekho.com/amp/articles/jee-advanced-2026-final-45-day-mock-test-and-toppers-revision-strategy/)
- Source rejected: a SATHEE (IIT Kanpur domain) page listing named rank-holders with precise numbers (e.g., "40% accuracy improvement", "60-70% of questions follow similar patterns") names AIR 1 holders that conflict with the CollegeDekho article (e.g., a different AIR 1 for 2023), so I did not use its specific claims. — [SATHEE](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/pyqs/jee-advanced-success-strategies) (unreliable, excluded)

### Inferences
- The Japanese protocol is the clearest, directly implementable template for "past papers": untimed first pass, timed per-section passes, repeat-until-three-clean, a shortened-time final run, and a mistakes-with-reasons notebook. It maps onto spaced re-testing very cleanly.
- Korea's policy of excluding "drill-advantaged" items is evidence that national exam designers see pure pattern-drilling as a problem; this argues for variation and transfer in the problems Toishi serves.
- Three independent traditions (Korean 만점자, JEE AIR 1, Chinese 694) converge on tagging error causes; the exact category names differ but all fit a small set: not known / known but misapplied / calculation or careless / misread / slow or inefficient.

### Gaps
- Not found: 青チャート (Aoi Chart, Suken Shuppan) structure and how top candidates use it; Japan's 共通テスト (common test) specifics; 2027학년도 수능 plan (exam in November 2026); Allen/FIITJEE/Physics Wallah test-series structure and analysis reports; JEE error-log practice beyond topper anecdotes.
- No Korean or Japanese study evaluating notebook effects was fetched.

## 4. Common principles and research evidence

### Takeaway
Retrieval practice (testing effect) and the value of attending to mistakes are well supported in general research; what is not supported in my retrieved sources is any controlled evaluation of the specific gaokao-style 错题本, 题海战术 vs targeted practice, or mock-exam schedules. Treat notebook rituals as promising folk practice, and treat the spaced retrieval of weak skills as the evidenced mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- [E] Hui, de Bruin, Donkers et al. (2021), Educational Psychology Review 33: retrieval practice beat restudy on a 7-day delayed test in both experiments (Exp. 1 d = .25; Exp. 2 d = .36); only about half of students benefited from retrieval practice; individual feedback showing students their own delayed-test result increased and sustained later retrieval-practice use in the group that benefited, while general instruction about the testing effect did not sustain. The paper is not about learning from errors and is not a Chinese sample. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-021-09604-x)
- [E, review in Chinese] Zhou et al. (2017), Journal of Psychological Science 40(4): retrieval practice improves long-term retention more than restudy even without feedback; effect is robust across materials, intervals and prior ability; spaced multiple tests, delayed feedback and harder retrieval formats increase the effect; low-stakes frequent quizzing may boost achievement and reduce test anxiety. This is a review article (no participants), and the abstract does not say the reviewed studies used Chinese students. — [ECNU Journal of Psychological Science](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2017/V40/I4/913)
- [E] Schleppenbach, Flevares, Sims & Perry (2007), Elementary School Journal 108(2): in 44 videotaped grade 1/4/5 math lessons, error rates were similar in China and the US; Chinese teachers more often asked follow-up questions about errors (used mistakes as prompts for inquiry), while US teachers commented more. Small, classroom-level, elementary; not an outcomes study. — [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/teachers-responses-to-student-mistakes-in-chinese-and-us-mathemat/)
- [A]/[F] Principles repeated by practitioners across countries: error causes labeled (Korean 만점자; geography teacher; JEE AIR 1 2023); timed passes of past papers (Kyogakusha guide; 曾竞; the 必刷题 round-three advice); mocks as diagnostics with analysis time at least equal to test time (JEE; 694 scorer); spaced review tiers (same day, weekly, monthly in the Beijing News piece; weekly redo in the 694 scorer's method); retiring or consolidating old entries to keep a notebook thin (杨春雪; ○×3 rule in 赤本 guide). — sources above.

### Inferences
- Mapping to what is evidenced vs folk: spaced retrieval of skills (evidenced); delayed feedback and harder retrieval formats (evidenced in review); cause-tagging, notebook thinness and weekly cadences (folk; plausible mechanisms, no tests found); mock-then-analyze (folk, consistent across countries).
- The Hui et al. finding that showing students their own retrieval-vs-restudy outcome changes strategy use suggests that surfacing each learner's own data (what they retained after a delay) may be a better motivator than lectures about learning science. This is a design inference; the study is a lab-style experiment with a Dutch-affiliated author team on non-exam materials.
- Because "only about half" benefited from retrieval practice in that study, a per-user feedback loop is more defensible than a one-size claim.

### Gaps
- No CNKI abstracts were retrievable; I found no controlled study of 错题本 effects on academic scores in Chinese high schools. A search returned several non-Chinese error-analysis papers and a Korean retrieval-practice paper listing, but I did not read them, so none are cited.
- No study evaluating mock-exam frequency, 一轮/二轮/三轮 allocation, or 题海 vs targeted practice was found.
- Background I did not verify this session (from model knowledge only; verify before citing): Roediger & Karpicke testing effect (2006); Metcalfe's 2017 Annual Review "Learning from Errors" (hypercorrection effect, errors with high confidence corrected more readily); interleaving research; FSRS algorithm documentation.

## 5. Implications for Toishi: adopt, adapt, avoid

### Takeaway
The best-supported design core is already in the brief (FSRS-scheduled fresh-variant retrieval of skills). The gaokao-style practices worth adding are a short error-cause taxonomy, blind redo with retirement rules, a past-paper protocol for practice tests, post-mock analysis by skill and error type, and low-friction mistake capture. What to avoid: transcription overhead, unreviewed logs, and claims of efficacy that borrow vendor numbers. Two tensions for the product owner: the app is English-only and Mac-only per the user's stated plan, and content is meant to be open-source only, which limits direct use by Chinese gaokao students and access to 真题.

(See Sections 1-4 for the evidence; the items below are my synthesis, not new sources.)

### Inferences

**ADOPT**
1. A small cause taxonomy captured at the moment of a miss, merged from the sources: didn't know the concept / knew it but misapplied (method) / calculation or careless / misread the question / guessed or unsure (even if correct) / correct but slow or inefficient method. (Korean 오답노트 labels; JEE conceptual/calculation/silly; 四改试卷 four types; geography teacher's list.) Tag "correct but guessed" and "correct but slow" as diagnostic signals, not only wrong answers (四改试卷 type b/d; CollegeDekho "skipped questions you could have solved").
2. Cheap-vs-rich mistake entries: slips get a one-line tag; real misunderstandings get a step-level diagnosis plus one "next-time" sentence (694 scorer; red-ink reflections; "one reminder sentence").
3. Blind redo with a fresh variant, not the same problem, so the redo tests the skill rather than memory of the answer (694 scorer's covered-solution redo; 母题 and variants; 举一反三 idea). The mistakes bank should schedule a redo at same-day, about one week and about one month (Beijing News three-stage review), with a "needs reinforcement" flag on repeat failure (an FSRS lapse/leech analog).
4. Retirement rule to keep the bank "thin": graduate an entry after three clean, timed, blind solves (赤本 ○×3 rule; 杨春雪's consolidation).
5. A past-paper/practice-test protocol: untimed first run, timed per-section runs, repeat until clean, a final shortened-time full run about a week later (Kyogakusha guide); allow a "10 minutes shorter" setting.
6. Post-test analysis as a first-class screen: errors by skill and by cause, time per question, solvable-but-skipped, and slowest topics; budget analysis time comparable to test time (JEE AIR 1 2023: 2-3 hours per paper).
7. Exam-goal rounds that echo 一轮/二轮/三轮: foundation (by skill), topic/method consolidation, then timed whole papers and boosters in the final stretch; no new topics in the last weeks (Korean 만점자; CollegeDekho).
8. Surface the learner's own delayed-retention data to motivate retrieval over rereading (Hui et al. 2021 finding that individual feedback sustained strategy change).

**ADAPT**
- Photo-to-错题本 capture and mistake printing: offline Mac app can substitute screenshot/paste import, Anki-style card import, and PDF export of selected mistakes for paper retesting (the Bilibili workflow of a timed paper from weak items). Handwriting-removal OCR is optional and heavy.
- Knowledge-point granularity: Squirrel AI claims >10,000 points for middle-school math [V]; choose granularity at the level where one skill can generate many distinct variants and a miss is diagnosable, not as fine as possible.
- Localized syllabus graphs (iFlytek's 33 provincial graphs) map to "exam profile" packs (an exam goal bundling a syllabus-to-skill map and typical section timings).
- Score and lost-points analysis of school platforms (好分数) maps to a "mock import" where the user enters per-question outcomes from an external paper and the app assigns them to skills.

**AVOID**
- Forcing hand transcription of questions or elaborate notebook rituals (>10 min per problem, >6 h/week reported by one student; 曾竞 says upkeep can exceed benefit).
- Passive logs with no scheduled re-test (the "后悔本" failure mode).
- Defaulting to unlimited volume (题海); cap daily new items and prefer many variants of weak skills, but allow volume for those who want it (姜彦文 says volume is needed).
- 押题 (predicted-question) content, per the geography teacher.
- Adopting vendor numbers (billions of questions, 1.12 billion knowledge points, Guinness-certified results) as evidence of effectiveness; the Squirrel AI Guinness experiment was company-funded and does not validate methodology, and AI-grading error reports exist. Plan to measure the app's own effect (retention at delay by cause tag) instead.
- Depending on cloud chatbots for solutions: major Chinese chatbots reportedly restrict photo-question solving during the gaokao period; an offline app with its own verified solutions avoids that dependency.

**Tensions to flag to the product owner**
- "Best for gaokao" likely requires Chinese-language content, 真题 access and school-mock import; the user's stated plan is English-only, open-source content only. Under that plan, the credible claim is "the method layer" (scheduling, error taxonomy, redo, analysis), not gaokao content coverage.
- Copyright status of gaokao 真题 for redistribution was not researched.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found that any Chinese app uses FSRS or equivalent spaced scheduling for problem banks; I did not verify this either way. Vendor feature pages mention push of similar and easy-to-err questions and "测-学-练" loops, which is adaptive practice but not documented as spaced repetition.
- No usage-rate data for how many gaokao students use 错题本 vs apps.
- I did not verify any open-licensed gaokao-level problem sources.
