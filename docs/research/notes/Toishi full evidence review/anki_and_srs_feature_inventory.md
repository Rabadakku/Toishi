# Anki and other spaced-repetition apps: feature inventory versus the Toishi spec

> **Reading guide.** Research date: 2026-11-01 (Anki 26.09.3 is the newest release seen). "ME" means the Toishi. ME statuses come from the local spec [`docs/spec.md`](/docs/spec.md) (requirement IDs such as QUE-9), the decision records in `/docs/decisions/` (ADR-0010, 0013, 0014, 0015, all dated 2026-11-01), `docs/card-format.md`, `docs/backlog.md`, and a presence check of the source tree (file and function names only, not a behavior test; other reviewers are checking the built system).
>
> **Status words:** **Have**, **Partial**, **Missing**, **Excluded** (deliberately, per the spec's "Not in 1.0" list and ARC-2), **N/A**. **Priority words in recommendations:** **P1** do for 1.0 if cheap, **P2** plan for 1.1, **P3** later or low value, **Skip** do not build.
>
> **Evidence ladder.** (1) vendor documentation and release notes (primary); (2) forum thread views, likes and post counts as a demand proxy (likes are not installs, and views reflect support traffic as much as popularity); (3) GitHub stars; (4) app-store aggregates. AnkiWeb add-on download counts are not public (Question 2). Reddit was not reachable.
>
> **Corrections to earlier material.** The scheduler audit (`/home/claude/reports/Spaced repetition algorithm gap audit.md`) predates ADR-0013 and ADR-0014. It says ME has no load balancer and lists flashcard bugs. ADR-0014 now builds a load balancer, Anki-style Easy Days, an own new-flashcard limit, same-day flashcard relearn and flashcard leech handling. ADR-0013 builds auto-fit, a health-check adoption gate, memory rebuild from the log and `check_database`. Where the spec text and an ADR disagree I give the ADR status and flag the drift (Question 4, Inferences).

## Question 1. What does Anki (25.x and 26.x, desktop plus AnkiMobile and AnkiDroid) offer today?

### Takeaway
Current Anki (desktop 26.09.x) is FSRS scheduling plus a very large option surface: presets, Easy Days, a load balancer, a workload simulator, filtered and custom study, a search-language browser, note types and templates, cloze, image occlusion, rich statistics (true retention, estimated total knowledge), media and database maintenance, backups, AnkiWeb sync and add-ons. Since 23.10 it has absorbed several formerly add-on features (image occlusion in 23.10, auto advance in 23.12, load balancing, Easy Days and true-retention stats in 24.11, sibling dispersal in 26.05). Mobile (AnkiMobile at $24.99, AnkiDroid free) adds tap and swipe gestures, a scratchpad and TTS (an AnkiMobile widget is a long-standing request with 91 likes), and several features exist only because of sync.

**Deck options and FSRS** — [Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/deck-options.md)
- Presets: Save, Add, Clone, Rename, Delete, Save to All Subdecks; changing a preset is not retroactive. Daily limits can be set at preset, this-deck and today-only scope; "New cards ignore review limit"; "Limits start from top".
- New-card gather orders (Deck, Deck then random notes, Ascending or Descending position, Random notes, Random cards), new-card sort orders, new/review mix; review sort orders include relative overdueness (SM-2) and ascending retrievability (FSRS).
- Burying: bury new, review and interday-learning siblings.
- Easy Days: Normal, Reduced or Minimum per weekday; works with FSRS and SM-2; "After the interval is calculated, it will be adjusted by a small amount to change the due date"; not retroactive; setting every day to Reduced or Minimum gives the same workload as all Normal.
- FSRS: desired retention is "the most important setting in FSRS" (default 90%, keep it below 97%); per-deck desired retention inside a preset; "Help Me Decide (Experimental)" workload simulator; Optimize and Optimize All Presets; "Check health when optimizing (slow)"; "Reschedule cards on change" defaults to off; steps shorter than a day recommended, empty steps let FSRS control short-term scheduling (experimental); CMRR removed in 25.07.
- Advanced: maximum interval, historical retention, ignore cards reviewed before, custom scheduling in JavaScript.
- Timers: 60 s internal cap, optional on-screen timer, time does not affect scheduling; Auto Advance (23.12+).
- Client compatibility: "Ensure all of your Anki clients support FSRS" (AnkiMobile 23.10, AnkiWeb, AnkiDroid 2.17+) — a sync-driven requirement.
- FSRS FAQ: re-optimize about monthly or whenever the review count doubles (Q4); using only Again and Good works fine and may be more accurate (Q2); FSRS ignores time spent (Q5); the optimizer works with any number of reviews since 24.06.3 (Q6); no optimal new-cards-per-day (Q7) — [FSRS FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/frequently-asked-questions-about-fsrs.md)

**Studying** — [Studying](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/studying.md)
- Again, Hard, Good, Easy on keys 1 to 4, Space or Enter for Good; fuzz cannot be disabled. The More menu holds Flag, Bury card or note, Reset card, Set Due Date, Suspend, Options, Card Info, Previous Card Info, Mark Note, Create Copy, Delete Note, Replay/Pause Audio, plus or minus 5 s, Record Own Voice, Replay Own Voice. Sibling rules: buried until the next day, only new and review siblings are buried, buried and suspended are mutually exclusive. "/" opens the Study Deck switcher.

**Filtered decks and Custom Study** — [Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/filtered-decks.md)
- Custom Study: increase today's new limit, increase today's review limit, review forgotten cards, review ahead, preview new cards, study by card state or tag. Manual filtered decks take a search, a limit and an order, with Build and Rebuild. Turning off "Reschedule cards based on my answers" gives preview/cram mode. The manual calls review-ahead "not appropriate for repeated use".
- The browser's Preview button walks cards in a chosen order "without altering the scheduling" — [Study in a particular order](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/study-in-a-particular-order.md)

**Browser and search** — [Browsing](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/browsing.md); [Searching](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/searching.md)
- Cards and Notes modes; sidebar with Saved Searches; Notes menu (Add/Remove Tags, Clear Unused Tags, Toggle Mark, Change Note Type, Find Duplicates, Find and Replace with regex, Manage Note Types); Cards menu (Change Deck, Set Due Date with ranges like `60-90` and a `!` suffix, Reset, Reposition with start/step/shift, Toggle Suspend, Flag, Info).
- Search language: `is:due/new/learn/review/suspended/buried`, `flag:0-7`, `prop:ivl/due/reps/lapses/ease/pos/s/d/r`, `added:`, `edited:`, `rated:`, `introduced:`, regex, cloze search, accent-insensitive search, custom data, object IDs; `tag:animal` also matches subtags such as `animal::mammal`.

**Editing, note types, cloze, image occlusion, templates** — [Adding/Editing](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/editing.md); [Field Replacements](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/templates/fields.md); [Card Generation](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/templates/generation.md)
- Duplicate check on add, sticky fields, Marked tag, Create Copy; cloze; Image Occlusion (23.10+) with rectangle, ellipse and polygon masks, "Hide All, Guess One" or "Hide One, Guess One", Header, Back Extra and Comments fields, group/align/zoom/translucency tools, orphaned cards removed with Empty Cards.
- Templates: type-in answer `{{type:Field}}` compares text only, one per card, single line, and "does not change how the card is answered"; `type:nc:` ignores diacritics and case; TTS per field; hint fields; ruby; reverse and conditional card generation.
- LaTeX needs an external install while MathJax is built in — [Math & Symbols](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/math.md)

**Statistics** — [Card Info, Graphs and Statistics](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/stats.md)
- Card Info (key I) shows the history including manual rows. Statistics (key T): Today, Future Due plus daily load, Calendar, Reviews, Card Counts, Review Time, Review Intervals, Card Ease, Card Stability / Difficulty / Retrievability (FSRS-only, "estimated total knowledge"), Hourly Breakdown, Answer Buttons, True Retention Table (mature means 21 days or more; first review of each day).

### Inferences
- Between 23.10 and 26.05 Anki absorbed most of what its popular add-ons did (image occlusion, auto advance, load balancing, Easy Days, true retention, forgetting curve, sibling dispersal). What it still leaves to add-ons or to nobody: postpone/advance as one-click tools, a break scheduler, printing, controller support, AI card creation, look-and-feel.
- For ME 1.0 (desktop, offline, no sync) the mobile-only and sync-required items above are irrelevant by design. The comparison that matters is the flashcard, queue, browser, stats and data-maintenance surface.
- Nothing in Anki has a concept of skills, generated problems, typed-math grading or error diagnosis, so those ME features have no Anki baseline. The comparison is about everything around them.

### Gaps
- Release notes came through model-paraphrased fetches of GitHub pages, not raw text. The 25.05, 25.06 and 25.09 notes were not read. The AnkiMobile 26.10 beta thread has no feature content ([thread 71167](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/71167)).
- AnkiMobile's App Store review text and AnkiDroid's Play Store review text were not read. The Play Store counts were extracted from page markup and are approximate.

## Question 2. What do Anki add-ons and forum demand show that core Anki lacks?

### Takeaway
AnkiWeb download counts are deliberately hidden, so demand has to be read from GitHub stars, forum thread views/likes and Anki's own absorption of add-on features. The loudest signals are: getting cards in quickly (AI generation, importers, image occlusion), review-order and backlog tools (FSRS Helper), a nicer look, and onboarding. The add-on system itself is a recognized pain point.

### Cited Findings
**What can and cannot be measured**
- Download counts used to be public and were removed because authors inflated them: "Download counts used to be public, and it lead to some deck/add-on authors deliberately downloading their own items multiple times to try to boost their apparent popularity" (dae, 2023-04-11) — [forum thread 29200](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/29200)
- The rating system is unchanged since 2013, votes are binary, there is manipulation (a deck with about 7,000 fake likes is mentioned), and the team says the listing is being improved; no rating counts for top add-ons were given — [forum thread 71257](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/71257) (2026-09-29)

### Inferences

## Question 3. What do the other leading spaced-repetition apps offer that Anki lacks?

### Takeaway
Other apps differ mainly in how they decide what to study (SuperMemo's priority queue, RemNote's folder priorities and exam scheduler, WaniKani's gated levels), in card authoring inside notes (Obsidian SR, RemNote, Mochi), and in light game or test layers (Quizlet). Most of their distinctive features are teaching- or content-side and are outside ME's practice-tool scope. A few small items (cramming without scheduling, a skip button, prerequisite gating, confidence input) are relevant and mostly already in ME in another form.

### Cited Findings
**SuperMemo**
- Feature list: element difficulty, leech hunting, a priority queue with auto-postpone and auto-sort, incremental reading and video, contents tree, Advance/Postpone/Spread, tasklists, review calendar, statistics, sleep analysis — [SuperMemo features](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/features)
- Incremental reading in minimum form: import, extract, cloze, read points, priority queue; Mercy and Postpone for overload; Advance — [Minimum definition of incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Minimum_definition_of_incremental_reading)
- Priority queue: priority 0 to 100% (low number means high priority), queue auto-sorted at day start, auto-postpone on the first launch each day, low-priority items may wait "for months or years", and a "Priorities missed" statistic; the page does not mention any automatic notice of what was left out — [SuperMemo priority](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/priority)
- Neural review: a subset review guided by link associations (Ctrl+F2); its effect on scheduling is not documented — [Neural review](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Neural_review)

**RemNote**
- Smart multi-line cards, hints, a Card Table with bulk edit of SRS data and leech finding, spoiler protection (related cards delayed), Edit SRS Data, folder priorities, a daily review limit, an Exam Scheduler (named only), FSRS versus SM-2 (the "20 to 30% less load" claim is the vendor's), PDF learning and annotation, Anki import with scheduler history, AI cards and quizzes, a lecture recorder, offline-first sync, desktop and mobile; no practice-all or cram mode mentioned — [RemNote spaced repetition](https://www.remnote.com/feature/ultimate-spaced-repetition)

**Mochi**
- Markdown cards, templates (diagram cards, conditional rendering, dynamic fields), decks with custom views, FSRS, cramming, reverse review, hidden text, tags and filters, sync and backup, import and export, an API, desktop plus mobile plus a web clipper (from the docs index only; deep docs and pricing could not be fetched) — [Mochi docs](https://mochi.cards/docs/)

**Mnemosyne**
- Modified early SM-2 with a 0 to 5 rating, plugins, sync, research data collection, an Android review client; latest release 2.11 on 2023-11-12 — [Mnemosyne (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemosyne_%28software%29)

**Obsidian Spaced Repetition**
- Inline cards `Q::A`, `:::` for reversible cards, multi-line with `?` and `??`; cloze via `==highlight==`, `**bold**` or `{{curly}}`; note-level `#review` queue; decks from `#flashcards` tags or folders; cramming mode; FSRS and SM-2; a stats page — [obsidian-spaced-repetition](https://github.com/st3v3nmw/obsidian-spaced-repetition)

**Quizlet**
- Learn is an adaptive sequence with Write and Spell inside it; subscriber extras are custom study paths and progress tracking; Test gives non-subscribers one practice test per set; also Flashcards, Match, Blast, Live and AI Practice Tests; the help article does not mention spaced repetition — [Quizlet study modes](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/articles/360030841732)

**Brainscape**
- A 1 to 5 confidence rating sets the interval; per-rating intervals are adjustable; timeline presets for cramming, everyday and long-term; automatic interleaving of older material — [Brainscape: the science of never forgetting](https://brainscape.com/academy/science-never-forgetting-spaced-repetition)

**WaniKani**
- Articles on level-up, reset level and reset reviews, skip and undo buttons, offline use, and widgets (Heat Map, Days Studied, Correct Percentage, Extra Study) — [WaniKani knowledge base](https://knowledge.wanikani.com/)
- Items move through SRS stages and Guru unlocks dependent items — [WaniKani SRS stages](https://knowledge.wanikani.com/wanikani/srs-stages/); a burnout and recovery discussion — [WaniKani community thread](https://community.wanikani.com/t/tips-for-recovering-from-burnout/54043)

**Execute Program**
- Executed prompts; prerequisite lessons must be successfully reviewed to unlock later lessons; short lessons; missed answers do not penalize unless the student gives up; progress favors new lessons over longer intervals — [Andy Matuschak's notes on Execute Program](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Execute_Program)

**Backlog recovery as a requested Anki feature**
- A forum proposal (2026-07-21) for a "backlog recovery planner to prevent user churn and burnout"; it has 98 views, 1 like and 2 posts, so it shows the idea exists, not that demand is large — [forum thread 70487](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/proposal-backlog-recovery-planner-to-prevent-user-churn-burnout/70487)

### Inferences
- SuperMemo and RemNote prioritize at the collection level (priority numbers, folder priorities). ME does it from goals, exam dates and recall (QUE-11, ENG-8, ENG-17), which is stronger for a student with deadlines.
- Cramming without touching the schedule is the one mode that three apps and Anki all offer (Anki preview/filtered deck, Obsidian SR, Mochi) and ME lacks (Question 4, B2).
- Execute Program's "no penalty for a miss" is a design fork from ME's "a miss stays a miss" (ADR-0014). ME's choice keeps FSRS honest, and "Guessed" plus slip handling already soften it.
- Quizlet's games and AI tests, RemNote's PDF and recorder, SuperMemo's incremental reading and sleep analysis, and Obsidian's note-centred cards all sit on the teaching or notes side of the line ME drew (practice, not teaching).

### Gaps
- WebSearch budget ran out mid-research. Not obtained: WaniKani userscript popularity, Mochi's deep docs and pricing, Mnemosyne's own feature page, Quizlet and Brainscape pricing and detail, Execute Program's actual intervals, RemNote pricing, and any user-count numbers for these apps. A follow-up message can continue the search.
- The "20 to 30% less load" RemNote claim and Brainscape's efficacy language are vendor statements, not independent evidence.

### Inferences
- **Where ME is thin.** The 14 Missing and 24 Partial rows cluster in three places. (1) Flashcards and imported Anki decks: Hard misuse (A20), reversed-pair siblings (A17), nested cloze (C3), `[latex]` and other import limits (C8, D16). (2) Study-screen escape hatches: an ungraded "Not now" (B7), practice without scheduling (B2). (3) 1.1 browser and stats polish: bulk actions, columns, saved filters, forgetting curve, knowledge over time (D1 to D7, E6). Everything about problems, skills, exams, backlog and typed math is ahead of Anki or has no Anki equivalent.
- **Rows that are Missing but correctly so.** A21 (interval labels), C5 (audio, until an English module), D5 (regex find and replace), D18 (other-app importers), E8 (hourly chart), G8 and C4 (image occlusion, until a pack needs it) have little or no evidence of value for a STEM practice tool.
- **Spec and ADR drift to fix** (spec and ADRs are both dated 2026-11-01; the ADRs describe what was decided or built for these items):
  1. ENG-12 still describes a manual "Fit my memory model" after 512 reviews; ADR-0013 I7 replaced it with `auto_fit` at 300 items, then every 30 days or when items double, with one-click revert.
  2. The load balancer and Easy Days exist only in ADR-0014 I3; the spec has a settings row "Easy days" without an ID and no balancer requirement. DEV-10 makes scheduling requirements Critical and traces each by ID (`pytest.mark.req`), and the Specify gate says every requirement has an ID, so a balancer or Easy Days behavior with no ID cannot be traced.
  3. `daily.new_flashcards` (default 20) is in ADR-0014 I5 but not in the spec's settings table; ADR-0014 also changes flashcard relearn (return at least 5 items later) and leech handling (not served) beyond the spec's ENG-9 and QUE-2 wording.
  4. The import limits in ADR-0015 (no Image Occlusion, `[latex]` as text, no suspension or flags, nested cloze pending) are absent from IMP-2 to IMP-8.
  5. The scheduler audit's gap table is stale against ADR-0013 and ADR-0014 (it lists the load balancer, flashcard relearn, flashcard parameters and the leech flag as open).
- **Design choices the evidence supports.** No presets (A1), outcome-derived ratings (A20), no overdue count (QUE-7) with a bounded wait (QUE-4), a single retention goal per course (A6), and automatic refitting (A7) each answer a heavily liked Anki complaint (threads 58773, 49092, 50051, 36715).
- **Flashcards are ME's weakest, most Anki-like surface.** The spec says flashcards "work like Anki", so they inherit Anki's known issues, and imported Anki decks bring sibling, cloze and LaTeX edge cases with them. They deserve a short parity pass because the Anki import screen (M8) will put them in front of users in 1.0.

### Gaps
- ME statuses rest on spec text, ADR text and a presence check of function and file names. I did not run the app or its tests. The 1.1 screens (browser, stats, settings) exist only as short spec text, so "Partial" there reflects spec brevity, not a built UI.
- I did not establish whether the load balancer is applied to flashcards, or whether imported reversed cards share a sibling key anywhere other than the importer.
- No Anki accessibility facts were gathered. AnkiDroid's own documentation (a separate site) was not read. App-store review text was not read.
- Popularity is missing for the add-ons listed under Question 2 Gaps, and for every non-Anki app (user counts, pricing). A follow-up search can fill these.

### Inferences
- Complaints 3, 9, 10, 11 and 15 are fixed by architecture (offline, no add-ons, no sync, bounded waits), so they are low-risk and cost nothing more to build.
- Complaints 1 and 2 are fixed only by intent. They carry some of the largest like counts found (thread 42555 at 514, thread 43692 at 256, thread 17510 at 219; only Hard misuse at 352 sits among them), so a cheap early user test is the best use of effort.
- Complaints 4 and 5 are fixed for problems and exposed for flashcards, which is where Anki users will judge ME first when they import a deck.
- Forum likes come from heavy users, so they overweight power-user concerns (review order, presets) relative to the casual student ME targets. The onboarding and look-and-feel signals are less biased.

- r/Anki was blocked (403), so there is no Reddit evidence. Forum, Hacker News and store data were the substitutes. Hacker News comments about "ugly UI", "steep learning curve" and backlog spirals were seen but their URLs were not retained, so they are not cited.
- App-store review text for AnkiMobile and AnkiDroid was not read, so complaint frequency in reviews is unknown.
- No survey data separates complaint frequency from loudness.
