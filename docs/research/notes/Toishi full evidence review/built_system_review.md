## Overall: does what was built make sense, and what should be fixed first?

### Takeaway
The data core is the strongest part and should be kept as is: storage, grader, FSRS adapter and the honest rating map. The weak spots are elsewhere. The verification gates that would let a non-coder trust it (M7: DEV-6 to DEV-11) are not built. There is no in-app restore. A few grading and scheduling edges still let a miss go unrated or a solution-aided answer count. Effort went into deep engine features before the user-facing 1.0 basics and the Calculus I content the release depends on.

### Inferences
- What exists fits the product's core promise (no lost progress, no wrong grading, honest schedules) at the data layer. Most risk now sits at the edges where flows meet: fit and recompute, undo after a solution, practice tests through the band rule, check cards through the day rule. These are exactly what the missing DEV-9 state machine and the trace or trust report would catch.
- Built early relative to value: shadow partial credit (ENG-7, needs about 200 credited explicit reviews before it can switch on), the weak-spot finder (30-day windows), calibration statistics with Wilson and Bonferroni intervals and held-out metrics (no stats screen yet), auto-fit with five time-ordered folds (first fit at 300 items), per-domain and per-Anki-group parameter sets, Easy Days parity with Anki's load balancer, find-the-error and which-method kinds, vacation mode and goal-change spreading. But they pay off only after weeks of data, add branches to every grade (the 871-line diagnosis hook runs on every final answer), and were built before onboarding, goals, exams, an import screen, restore, plots and any Calculus I card.

## Architecture: does the layering follow AGENTS.md and the ADRs?

### Takeaway
Yes on every hard rule: core has no GUI imports, a single pywebview importer, no eval, an offline loopback server, one SQLite writer and per-thread readers. The blur is inside core: the queue service also grades, plans tests, manages goals and vacation and runs the fit thread, and the engine also owns fitting, deck groups, repair and placement.

### Cited Findings
- `lint-imports` (run here): 3 contracts kept, 0 broken: core has no app or GUI toolkit; neither core nor app imports anki; only app.shell imports pywebview ([pyproject.toml:107-129](/pyproject.toml)).
- Only [app/shell.py:20-23](/app/shell.py) imports `webview`. pywebview's eval-wrapped result delivery is replaced with `run_js` ([app/shell.py:187-195](/app/shell.py)). The pywebview contract's source list is narrower than the rule (L6).
- No `eval`, `exec`, `compile`, `sympify`, `parse_expr` or `lambdify` appears in core or app (grep), and the guard tests cover both Python and web code (`tests/test_no_eval.py`: `test_our_python_never_evaluates_strings`, `test_our_web_code_never_evaluates_strings`).
- SQLite threading follows AGENTS rule 6. The single writer thread `mastery-db-writer` runs every write job in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE ... COMMIT`, and the caller blocks on `future.result()` ([core/storage/connection.py:191-293](/core/storage/connection.py)). Nested writes become savepoints ([connection.py:228-241](/core/storage/connection.py)). Readers are per-thread with a busy lock, and closing waits for other threads' reads, per ADR-0018 ([connection.py:303-322, 445-458](/core/storage/connection.py)). Every connection sets `fullfsync` and `checkpoint_fullfsync` ([connection.py:58-74](/core/storage/connection.py)).
- Bridge calls are serialized by `_serial`, an RLock wrapper that maps `QueueError` to `UserMessage` ([app/api.py:169-180, 196](/app/api.py)). StudyService has its own RLock, and the auto-fit thread takes only that one ([core/queue/session.py:196, 650-665](/core/queue/session.py)) (L10).
- Measured sizes: `core/queue/session.py` is 3,279 lines, with StudyService at lines 179-3040 (2,862 lines, 84 methods), plus `_CallEngine` and `_Light`. It has 22 `with self._locked()` entry points and 23 `self.engine()` calls, and each `engine()` builds two Engines plus a DiagnosisHook ([session.py:208-224](/core/queue/session.py)). `core/engine/service.py` is 1,099 lines, with Engine at 170-1038 (64 methods). `core/engine/diagnosis/hook.py` is 871 lines; `core/storage/user_store.py` is 873 lines (UserStore has 82 methods); `app/api.py` is 573 lines (37 Api methods). Core totals 35,894 lines, app 1,658, web/src 7,757, tests 25,956 (measured with wc).

- A final answer is saved in the session row at submit and rated on continue or diagnose (ADR-0009), committing the attempt, FSRS event and session row in one transaction ([app/api.py:463-480](/app/api.py); [core/queue/session.py:1660-1691, 1889-1955](/core/queue/session.py); [core/storage/user_store.py:390-447](/core/storage/user_store.py)).

### Inferences
- The "commit every graded answer immediately" rule (AGENTS rule 5, ARC-8) is met in substance: the submitted answer is durable in the session row, and resume grades it, at its original time even after a rollover. But the attempt log lags until continue. That is a documented deviation, and the kill loop (DEV-8) should explicitly SIGKILL on the verdict screen.
- The layering is right, but the god-objects raise the cost and risk of every C2 change, because an agent must load around 3k lines to edit the queue. Splitting along the existing banners would not change the architecture.

- I did not trace `core/queue/snapshot.py`, `planner.py` or `share.py` line by line; they were assessed through their tests and constants only.

## Critical flows traced end to end against the spec

### Takeaway
Answer-to-commit, undo, give-up, resume, backup and the Anki history replay are implemented carefully and tested. The weak edges are practice tests (M2, M3), undo after a shown solution (M4), checks through the day rule (M5), fit atomicity (M1), the restore path (H1), and holds that never settle (L1).

### Cited Findings
- **Answer, grade, rating, FSRS, commit.** `submit` ([core/queue/session.py:1214-1359](/core/queue/session.py)) never counts a syntax or shape error ([1287-1297](/core/queue/session.py)). A non-final miss is logged at once through `record_try` ([1319-1350](/core/queue/session.py)). A final answer goes to `_hold`, which saves the session row ([1660-1691](/core/queue/session.py)). `next` and `diagnose` lead to `_finalize` ([1731-1817, 1889-1955](/core/queue/session.py)), then `Engine.grade` ([core/engine/service.py:343-363](/core/engine/service.py)), then `_grade` ([core/engine/grading.py:222-488](/core/engine/grading.py)), then `rate` ([core/engine/rating.py:150-211](/core/engine/rating.py)), then `record_answer`. That last step writes the attempt, state snapshots, review events, credits and session row in one transaction ([core/storage/user_store.py:390-447](/core/storage/user_store.py)). Tests: SIGKILL at random moments ([tests/storage/test_fault_injection.py:113-139](/tests/storage/test_fault_injection.py)); `test_resume_after_a_crash_continues_the_same_session` and `test_a_crash_between_grading_and_replanning_is_recovered` ([tests/queue/test_simulation.py:90, 127](/tests/queue/test_simulation.py)).

## Security: imported HTML, media route, CSP, bridge

### Takeaway
The defenses match ADR-0012 and ADR-0017 and are layered: a strict CSP, two independent sanitizers, a media route that validates names, and loopback by number. The DEV-14 negative suite (an XSS corpus and an explicit `window.pywebview` test) is still to come in M11.

### Cited Findings
- CSP: `script-src 'self'` with no inline scripts and no eval; `style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'`; `img-src`, `media-src`, `font-src` and `connect-src` limited to 'self'; `object-src`, `frame-src`, `base-uri` and `form-action` set to none ([web/index.html:11-12](/web/index.html)).
- Media route ([app/media.py](/app/media.py)): pack ids must match `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,127}` (line 39), and pack names and user files are validated before lookup (lines 118-135). It refuses symlinks (line 250), allows GET and HEAD only, and sends `nosniff` and a `default-src 'none'; sandbox` CSP on responses (per ADR-0017; tests in `tests/app/test_media.py`).
- Imported HTML is sanitized in core and again by a DOM copy in the page (`web/src/lib/anki-html.ts`), with the allow-lists compared by `tests/app/test_anki_allowlist.py`. A page test injects 7 script vectors (onerror, script, onclick, `javascript:`, svg onload) and asserts nothing runs and no handler attributes survive ([tests/ui/test_anki_cards.py:30-35, 265-281](/tests/ui/test_anki_cards.py)).
- The bridge surface is 29 public methods (37 methods in the class), each taking at most one JSON object, type-checked and capped ([app/api.py:120-160, 280-558](/app/api.py)). Item calls are guarded by `item_key`. `report_self_test` only acts in self-test runs ([app/api.py:299-304](/app/api.py)). The offline tripwire tests are in `tests/test_offline.py`.

### Inferences
- With script-src 'self' and both sanitizers, card content reaching `window.pywebview` (HAZ-4) needs a CSP bypass plus a sanitizer bypass. The main residual surface is CSS (`unsafe-inline` styles from note types), which is mitigated by scoping and `contain: layout`.

### Gaps
- No full XSS corpus, and no test that card content can't call `window.pywebview.api` (DEV-14, M11). I did not audit `core/importers/anki/sanitize.py` (502 lines) rule by rule.

## Build and scripts

### Takeaway
check.sh's fast, full and app tiers match DEV-1. The Mac runner is safe, packaging follows ADR-0001 (arm64, ad-hoc signed, debug off, anki excluded), and the self-test drives the real bridge. What's missing are the M7 steps: test protection, trace, trust report and version guard.

### Cited Findings
- [scripts/check.sh:1-80](/scripts/check.sh). `fast` runs ruff, mypy, import-linter and pytest without UI or slow tests. `full` adds the web check and build plus Playwright (WebKit on the Mac, Chromium elsewhere). `app` adds `build.sh` and the .app `--self-test`. The pre-commit hook runs `check.sh fast` ([scripts/git-hooks/pre-commit](/scripts/git-hooks/pre-commit)). There is no trace, trust report or test-protection step (DEV-6, 10, 11).
- The Mac runner accepts only `setup`, `check`, `spike` or `all` from request files, never runs request content, keeps a single lock and takes over a stale one ([scripts/mac-runner.sh:20-77](/scripts/mac-runner.sh)); tests are in `tests/test_scripts.py`.
- `build.sh` compiles the shipped packs with full checks (keys self-checked, KaTeX through node), runs PyInstaller, ad-hoc signs, and verifies with `--deep --strict` ([scripts/build.sh:1-38](/scripts/build.sh)). The spec excludes anki and aqt, sets `debug=False` and targets arm64 ([packaging/Toishi.spec:42-64](/packaging/Toishi.spec)).
- The self-test creates a profile, attaches the Test pack, sets a section to Learning now, starts a session, answers one right and one wrong, undoes, and ends ([app/main.py:125-200](/app/main.py)) (L9).
- Pins: `fsrs==6.3.2`, `sympy==1.14.0`, `pywebview==6.2.1`, `fsrs-rs-python==0.9.3` (no license, flagged for before any sharing), `hypothesis==6.170.14` ([pyproject.toml:9-29](/pyproject.toml)). The Python runtime here is 3.14.10 with SQLite 3.53.1.

### Inferences
- The build chain is fit for a personal Mac app. Its blind spot is that no script tells the owner what is proven, which is what DEV-11's trust report is for.

### Gaps
- The `.app` build and the WebKit runs can't execute on Linux; I relied on the backlog's Mac results (43.2 MB build, 478 ms median launch, both self-tests passing; [docs/backlog.md](/docs/backlog.md) "Verified on the Mac").
