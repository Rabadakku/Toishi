# Reach, platforms, distribution, community, translation, sustainability and evaluation for a free, non-commercial STEM practice app (Toishi)

Research date: 2026-11-01. Scope: how a one-person, no-budget, AI-agent-built, free offline app can reach and serve the most students, and how to prove it is better than alternatives. All StatCounter figures are page-view shares from the sites that run StatCounter's tracker (not user counts, and not student-specific). "Inference" bullets are my own reasoning or arithmetic from the cited numbers; they are not sourced facts.

Local project facts used throughout (so the writer does not have to re-open them): Release 1.0 is "macOS only (Apple silicon)", Windows and Linux are listed as non-goals, the web layer is Svelte plus KaTeX plus MathLive in a pywebview/WKWebView shell over a pure-Python core, packs are data-only `.mepack` zips, and a "Chinese interface and content for gaokao users" item sits on the Later list pending translation and a check of which past papers may be redistributed — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md) (sections "Release 1.0 scope and non-goals", "Later", PACK-3/7/9, open-decision table); [ADR-0001](/docs/decisions/0001-stack-and-shell-spike.md). The spec records "no GitHub" and "no paid CI" as decided, and lists a $99/year Developer ID as an open decision — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md) (DEV-2 and the "Risks and open decisions" table). Project constraints at the time: no paid services; building and testing on a Mac; no GitHub; English-only for now; the shipped version contains only open-licensed problems so it can be distributed free; and the bar is a system good enough that someone preparing for the gaokao could use it and come out on top. The spec adds that 1.0 excludes mobile apps, Windows and Linux, and anything online ("The app never opens a network connection"), that milestones M0-M6 are built (M7 is next), and that the card format was frozen at the end of M2.

## 1. What devices do target students actually use, and what fraction could use a Mac-only (Apple silicon) app?

### Takeaway
A Mac-only app reaches roughly 1 in 8 US page views, about 3% in China and about 1% in India (page-view proxies), and essentially none of the school-managed Chromebook population. Phones are the majority device in India (77% of page views) and worldwide (59%), and on PCs Windows dominates China (92% of desktop page views) and India (81%), so a Mac-only 1.0 serves the owner but not "students everywhere" or gaokao candidates.

### Inferences
- Rough Mac reach, computed as (Mac share of desktop) x (desktop share of all page views); a proxy, not a student count:

| Region | Desktop share of page views | Mac (OS X + macOS) share of desktop | Mac as share of all page views | Mobile share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US (Oct 2026) | 52.4% | 24.43% | about 12.8% | 45.43% |
| China (Sep/Oct 2026) | 46.21% | 5.87% | about 2.7% | 52.56% |
| India (Sep/Oct 2026) | 22.1% | 5.92% | about 1.3% | 77.3% |
| World (Sep; Mac share from Jun) | 39.26% | 16.37% | about 6.4% | 58.99% |

- Windows by the same arithmetic: about 34% of US, 43% of China and 18% of India page views; Android phones are about 73% of India page views (77.3% x 94.84%). So Windows (desktop) plus Android/iPad (touch) plus a browser path covers nearly everyone; Mac-only covers a small minority outside the US.
- The real Mac-addressable share is lower than the table: 1.0 needs Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later (ADR-0001), and I found no data on how much of the Mac installed base that excludes.
- For US high schoolers on school-issued Chromebooks, a desktop installer is effectively unavailable (managed devices block Linux/desktop installs, per the Anki forum anecdote), so only a web app or an Android app can reach them. About 88% of teens have a home computer but that falls to 78% below $30k household income, and phones are the one device 95% have.
- For gaokao candidates: between about a third and about two-thirds of Chinese minor netizens use a desktop or laptop (33.2% desktop and 31.7% laptop in 2022, overlap unknown), and on desktops Windows is 92% of page views, while phones (91%) and tablets (46%) are the most-used devices. The 2021 circular, as reported in English, tells primary and middle school students not to bring phones into classrooms (I did not check how it applies to senior high schools), so at least some phone study happens at home. A China-capable product therefore needs a touch-friendly web/PWA path and a Windows path before a Mac path.
- India's pattern (shared family phones, 27-38% own their phone, Android 95%) implies short sessions on a shared Android device, so small download size, offline-first and multiple local profiles matter more than desktop polish.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 authoritative breakdown of US K-12 school-issued devices by OS (searches returned only aggregator pages or 2015-2023 data).
- StatCounter is general web traffic, not students, and overweights heavy users; no student-specific OS share found.
- No gaokao-candidate-specific device survey; CNNIC's minors' data is for 2022 and a newer edition was not found.
- No data on Apple silicon share of Macs in use, or macOS version share.
- ASER 2024 computer-access figures not retrieved; Africa and Latin America not covered.
- Whether third-party apps can be installed on closed 学习机 (learning tablets) was not researched.

## 2. Which platform options reach more students from one codebase, and what did Anki do?

### Takeaway
Anki's reach came from a Rust backend that the desktop app and the AnkiDroid port both use (I could not confirm the same for AnkiMobile), a paid iOS app that funds the project, a volunteer-run free Android port, and a web service for sync. For a one-person, no-budget, Python-core project the cheapest broad-reach move is a web/PWA build running the existing pure-Python core in Pyodide (about 17 MB raw / about 11 MB compressed for Python plus SymPy), with a Windows pywebview build as the second step and native iOS/Android only if the PWA proves inadequate.

### Cited Findings
**What Anki did**
- Most backend logic lives in a Rust library (rslib); Python (pylib) wraps it through a private bridge; the desktop GUI is PyQt (aqt); new web UI is TypeScript/HTML in `ts/`; a protobuf definition describes the backend interface — [Anki developer docs, Architecture](https://dev-docs.ankiweb.net/en/latest/architecture.html)
- AnkiDroid consumes the desktop Rust backend through a JNI bridge built as an Android library (.aar), with Anki desktop included as a git submodule; the repo is GPL-3.0 and the Anki submodule AGPL-3.0; backend versions track Anki releases — [Anki-Android-Backend README](https://github.com/ankidroid/Anki-Android-Backend). The architecture page does not mention AnkiMobile, and I could not confirm from Anki's docs that AnkiMobile shares the Rust core (the task brief asserts it).
- AnkiMobile is paid, described by its author as "my way of putting food on the table and supporting future development" alongside the free desktop app and sync service; AnkiDroid is free and "developed by a different group of people" who based it on the desktop version; price cited as about $25 USD — [Anki FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/why-does-ankimobile-cost-more-than-a-typical-mobile-app.md)
- AnkiWeb sync "is intended for individual users", teachers should not bulk-create student accounts, and AnkiWeb will not sign data-processing agreements; AnkiMobile has volume licensing for academic institutions — [Anki FAQ, company or school](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/can-i-use-anki-in-a-company-or-school)

**One-codebase options**
- pywebview's home page says it is compatible with Windows, macOS, Linux and Android — [pywebview](https://pywebview.flowrl.com/). Its install guide: on Windows it needs pythonnet and the WebView2 Runtime (Chromium) installed; macOS uses PyObjC and WebKit; Linux uses GTK (WebKit2 4.1) or Qt; for Android it offers an `android` extra and points to Kivy packaging with little detail — [pywebview installation](https://pywebview.flowrl.com/guide/installation.html)
- BeeWare Briefcase lists macOS (.app), Windows, Linux (AppImage/Flatpak/system packages), iOS (Xcode project), Android (Gradle project) and static web output as targets, with a mix of CI-tested and untested entries per platform — [Briefcase platform support](https://briefcase.beeware.org/en/stable/reference/platforms/index.html)
- Pyodide's current stable line (314.x, Python 3.14.2) can be self-hosted from any static host that sets the WASM MIME type and CORS headers; the "full" distribution is 200+ MB but the core files are far smaller; a core tarball exists for minimal setups — [Pyodide, downloading and deploying](https://pyodide.org/en/stable/usage/downloading-and-deploying.html)
- My measurement on 2026-11-01 from the jsDelivr Pyodide CDN (v314.0.7): `pyodide.asm.wasm` 9,598,218 bytes raw (3,438,516 bytes with brotli/gzip), `python_stdlib.zip` 2,545,637 bytes, `sympy-1.14.0` wheel 4,185,567 bytes, `mpmath-1.4.1` wheel 430,985 bytes; the lock file lists 357 packages including sympy 1.14.0, mpmath 1.4.1, numpy and zstandard — [Pyodide CDN](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/pyodide/v314.0.7/full/) (lock file: [pyodide-lock.json](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/pyodide/v314.0.7/full/pyodide-lock.json)). The repo's ADR pins mpmath 1.3.0 because sympy 1.14.0 declares `mpmath<1.4` — [ADR-0001](/docs/decisions/0001-stack-and-shell-spike.md)
- Safari/WebKit deletes a site's script-writable storage (IndexedDB, localStorage, service-worker registrations and caches) after 7 days of Safari use without user interaction with the site; web apps added to the home screen are outside Safari, count their own days of use, and WebKit says deleting their first-party data is not intended — [WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/). MDN: browsers treat origin storage as best-effort by default; `navigator.storage.persist()` requests persistence, which Safari and Chromium decide automatically from engagement history and Firefox prompts for — [MDN storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)
- MathLive ships 15 core UI locales including zh-cn, zh-tw, ja, ko, ar and he plus 18 loadable on demand (secondary source) — [DeepWiki on MathLive localization](https://deepwiki.com/arnog/mathlive/8-localization)

### Inferences
- Ranking by reach per unit of effort for this project: (1) a web/PWA build of the existing core (one artifact covers Windows, Mac, Linux, school Chromebooks if the URL is allowed, iPad, Android phones; no installer, no signing, no store); (2) a Windows pywebview build (the spec already isolates the shell in `app/shell.py`, so only the shell, packaging and a Windows test environment are new); (3) native iPad/Android wrappers only if (1) shows a blocking limitation.
- What makes the Pyodide route plausible is the layering the spec already enforces: a GUI-free pure-Python `core/` (ARC-1, checked by import-linter), data-only packs (PACK-3), `app/shell.py` as the only module that imports pywebview, and a page that reaches the core only through a bridge (`app/api.py`) — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md), [AGENTS.md](/AGENTS.md). What does not port directly: the loopback server on 127.0.0.1 (ARC-2) and the pywebview JS bridge (would become messages to a Web Worker running Pyodide); the separate CAS worker process (ARC-6; would become a second Worker with a kill timer); `fsrs-rs-python` (Rust wheel; the spec already flags its missing license, and fitting would need a pure-Python or JS fallback); SQLite on disk (ARC-9 forbids browser storage today because WKWebView keeps none, and a web build reverses that, needing an IndexedDB- or OPFS-backed store plus mandatory export/import and a new ADR); the `mpmath` pin (1.3.0 in the repo vs 1.4.1 in Pyodide); and the spec's "Anything online" non-goal and ARC-2 ("no network"), because a hosted PWA needs a network for first load and updates, so the web build would have to be defined as a separate target that is offline after install, with no accounts and no telemetry. Each is a testable question, not a known blocker.
- Spike design to decide quickly and cheaply: run the existing grader golden suite (MATH-11: at least 600 cases) and the scheduler golden vectors inside Pyodide in headless Chromium and WebKit via the Playwright setup the repo already has; pass if results are identical and p95 grading latency and cold-load time on a mid-range Android phone are acceptable. If the spike fails, fall back to Windows pywebview plus a TypeScript port of only the numeric answer-check path for mobile.
- Persistence risk mitigation for a web build: request persistent storage, make "installed app" the recommended mode on iOS (exempt from the 7-day rule), and ship a visible, periodic "download backup file" prompt, because the project's data-integrity bar (acknowledged writes never lost) is higher than browser storage guarantees.
- Anki's lesson for sequencing is not "build Rust"; it is "keep the engine UI-free so every shell is thin", which the spec's ARC-1 import-linter rule already enforces.

### Gaps
- No benchmark of SymPy/Lark/py-fsrs performance in WebAssembly, no confirmation that Pyodide's sqlite3 can persist to IndexedDB/OPFS, and no check that py-fsrs installs in Pyodide.
- WebView2 Runtime presence on typical Windows 10/11 student machines and the Windows build/CI route (the spec says building and testing run on a Mac with no paid CI) were not researched.
- pywebview-on-Android maturity is unverified (docs say little).
- Anki docs do not confirm AnkiMobile uses the shared Rust core.

## 3. Distribution for a free app: Mac App Store vs direct download, Microsoft Store, fee waivers, and what is practical with no budget

### Takeaway
Apple's fee waiver is for legal entities (nonprofits, accredited schools, governments) and explicitly excludes individuals and sole proprietors, so a solo student pays $99/year for Developer ID/notarization or ships unnotarized with a manual "Open Anyway" step; Microsoft Store registration is free for individuals as of 2026-10-29 and MSIX packages are signed by Microsoft at no cost; and in China, app-store listing requires an ICP filing, so direct download and web are the realistic channels.

### Cited Findings
**Apple**
- Standard Apple Developer Program fee is $99/year (local currency where available). Waiver eligibility: nonprofit officially recognized by a relevant authority, accredited educational institution, or government entity; individuals, sole proprietors and single-person businesses are excluded; the entity must not have signed the Paid Applications Agreement or sell digital goods through its apps; no refunds on previously paid memberships — [Apple fee waiver](https://developer.apple.com/support/membership-fee-waiver/)
- A free Apple Account allows installing and testing on your own devices via Xcode (Personal Team limits: 3 devices, provisioning that expires after 7 days) but not distribution; distributing apps needs the paid Program or Enterprise — [Apple, compare memberships](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/). The fetched text did not clearly confirm in which tier Developer ID and notarization sit (the checkmarks were missing); the project's spec assumes the $99 program.
- Opening an app that is not notarized or is from an unidentified developer on recent macOS: try to open it, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > "Open Anyway" and confirm again; the page does not mention Control-click Open and warns about unsigned software; options may be unavailable on managed Macs — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102445)
- Mac App Store rules (Guideline 2.4.5): app sandbox; packaged with Xcode technologies (no third-party installers); updates only through the store; no downloading code that changes features; no license screens or keys — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Guideline 2.5.2 bars downloaded code that changes features (narrow exception for apps that teach executable code).
- Homebrew 5.0.0 (Nov 12, 2025) deprecates casks without codesigning and states it will disable casks failing Gatekeeper checks in September 2026 — [Homebrew 5.0.0](https://brew.sh/2025/11/12/homebrew-5.0.0/). I did not verify that the September 2026 disabling actually happened.

**Microsoft / Windows**
- Microsoft Store developer registration has no fee for individual accounts (and none for company accounts) under the new onboarding flow at storedeveloper.microsoft.com; individual accounts are for hobbyist, school and small non-commercial projects, with government ID and selfie verification; page updated 2026-10-29 — [Microsoft Learn, open a developer account](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/partner-center/open-a-developer-account)
- MSIX packages submitted to the Store are re-signed by Microsoft after certification at no cost; MSI/EXE installers must be Authenticode-signed beforehand and are not re-signed; Azure Artifact Signing costs about $9.99/month, is available to individuals in the USA and Canada only, and is not Store-eligible; OV certificates cost about $150-$300/year with keys on hardware since June 2023; EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen since 2024; all signed files start with SmartScreen warnings and build reputation over consecutive releases — [Microsoft Learn, code signing options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options)
- SignPath Foundation offers free code signing for qualifying open-source projects: all components under OSI-approved licenses with no commercial dual-licensing, actively maintained, already released, documented; the certificate is issued to SignPath Foundation as publisher; manual review, MFA and per-release approval required — [SignPath terms](https://signpath.org/terms)

**Android, China**
- New personal Google Play developer accounts created after Nov 13, 2023 need at least 12 testers opted in to a closed test for 14 consecutive days before applying for production access; the fetched page does not state the registration fee — [Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)
- Android developer verification timeline: in August (2026) a limited-distribution account launches, letting hobbyists and students share apps with up to 20 devices without a government ID or developer fee; on September 30, 2026 registration requirements begin in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand (the initial rollout covers apps from several major stores, including Google Play); the rest of 2026 stays limited while Google collects feedback; in 2027 verification expands globally to certified Android devices; installing from unverified developers will need an advanced sideloading flow or ADB, with a mandatory 24-hour lock — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-sideloading-changes-timeline-3679204/)
- China's MIIT notice (Aug 2023) extends ICP filing to apps and mini programs: "All apps and mini programs which are connected to the Internet are captured by the filing requirement"; operators were expected to file between September 2023 and March 2024 (the grace period ended 31 March 2024 and MIIT review began in April 2024); Xiaomi, Vivo and Huawei set earlier deadlines (mid-December 2023) with removals from early January 2024, and Vivo reported removing over 700 unfiled apps; Apple rejects new apps without an ICP number; failure to file can bring a fine of up to RMB 50,000 (about USD 7,000); AppInChina's Rich Bishop is quoted that foreign developers will need a Chinese entity or a local publisher; the article does not address individual developers, non-commercial exemptions, or offline apps beyond saying Internet-connected apps are covered — [Linklaters, May 2024](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102j72l/china-the-new-app-filing-regime-keeps-mobile-apps-under-tight-scrutiny)

### Inferences
- A no-budget plan, in order: (1) web/PWA on any free static host (zero signing, zero store, URL works on Chromebooks, iPad, Android, Windows, Linux, Mac); (2) Windows via Microsoft Store MSIX (free account, free Microsoft signing) or, for a direct EXE, SignPath Foundation (requires an OSI-approved app license and a public project — which also forces the pending "app license" decision); (3) Mac direct download of the ad-hoc-signed build with a one-page "Open Anyway" instruction, a published SHA-256, and honest wording; (4) pay $99/year only if Mac install friction measurably blocks users after the web version exists.
- Because the fee waiver needs a legal entity that is a nonprofit or accredited school, the only fee-free Apple route is to have such an entity hold the account (for example the owner's college or a fiscal host); whether either would agree, or whether a 501(c)(6) fiscal host qualifies, is untested.
- The Mac App Store is a poor fit for a PyInstaller/Python app (sandbox, Xcode packaging, store-only updates) and costs the same $99; skip it.
- A Mac build downloaded by others carries the quarantine flag, so unlike the owner's self-built copy it will hit Gatekeeper's "Open Anyway" path; and Homebrew distribution is closed to unsigned builds.
- Android: a PWA install avoids Play's 12-testers/14-days gate and the developer-verification regime that targets APK sideloading; this is an inference, since the sources describe app installs, not PWAs.
- China: avoid app-store listing (ICP filing, local entity); use direct-download installers (Windows), a PWA/web build hosted where reachable, and a Chinese-speaking partner for mirrors. Desktop installers are not clearly covered by the app filing rules, but the Linklaters article does not say.

### Gaps
- Apple's own notarization documentation did not load; the paid-program requirement for Developer ID/notarization is not confirmed from the fetched Apple text.
- Google Play's registration fee is not on the fetched help page.
- No data on antivirus/SmartScreen false-positive rates for PyInstaller Windows builds (only an unread forum hit), nor how long SmartScreen reputation takes to build.
- No research on free static hosting terms, Chinese Android store requirements for individuals, F-Droid, or Linux packaging.

## 4. Content-sharing ecosystems: AnkiWeb, AnKing/AnkiHub, the OER community, and letting teachers and students contribute packs safely

### Takeaway
AnkiWeb shows what an open upload model costs: no editorial review, thumbs-up/down ratings that can be gamed, and copyright policed by rights-holder notice; AnkiHub/AnKing and the WeBWorK Open Problem Library show the alternative, a gated model where an owner or editorial board approves changes into a curated tier. Toishi can do better than both because packs are data-only and every answer key can be auto-verified, so cheap automatic gates replace most human review.

### Cited Findings
**AnkiWeb shared decks**
- Shared decks are rated thumbs up/down; a reviewer can switch but not delete a rating and can rewrite a review after the creator replied; there is no moderation described in the thread, and no total deck count is given — [Anki forums, options for reviewing shared decks](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/options-for-review-shared-decks/26741.md)
- Ratings and written reviews affect a deck's sorting and reviewing needs only a login; AnkiWeb does not track which user downloaded which deck (the developer cited resource and privacy costs); in one thread a user reported that a deck received many ratings in a short time, some signed with another user's name, though the allegation was disputed and the poster later apologized to the deck's reviewer; proposed fixes (limit ratings to logged-in downloaders, a report button, rating caps, blocking new accounts) were discussed, a forum moderator argued fake reviews "cannot really be prevented, they can only be dealt with", and the thread shows no final decision or implemented change — [Anki forums, fake reviews](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/fake-reviews-on-ankiweb/45192)
- Rights-holders can ask to be notified of newly uploaded decks matching keywords; shared decks have a 24-hour waiting period before publication — [Anki forums, copyright checks](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/how-is-my-shared-deck-checked-by-copyright-holders/66227). Decks vanish if the author removes them or while hidden pending a copyright check — [Anki FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/a-deck-i-want-has-disappeared-from-ankiweb.md)
- The Terms require that a person sharing a deck "assert that it is entirely your own work" or hold a license from the rights holder; when a user asked about uploading a deck they had converted from a UWorld-style copycat question bank, a forum moderator said the team cannot advise on copyright but that, as described, the deck would not be allowed under the Terms — [Anki forums, question-bank copyright](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/copyright-question-about-uploading-a-question-bank-deck-to-ankiweb/70241). Anki's FAQ says users own the cards they create and may license or sell them, Anki has no DRM, and copyright holders can request removal of infringing shared decks from AnkiWeb — [Anki FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/can-i-use-anki-in-a-company-or-school)
- Anki's own docs call built-in collaboration limited (spreadsheets, taking turns) and name the AnkiHub and AnkiCollab add-ons — [Anki FAQ, collaboration](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/how-can-i-work-with-someone-else-to-create-a-deck.md)

**AnkiHub / AnKing**
- AnkiHub: members subscribe to a shared deck and submit suggestions; the deck creator or admin approves them before they reach subscribers; the site lists "hundreds" of decks, the AnKing Step Deck at "over 30k flashcards", free community decks, and paid tiers (Core about $6/month, Premium about $10/month, with inconsistent lifetime and yearly figures on the page); quality claims ("largest, most up to date") are the company's own — [AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net/). AnkiHub's detailed docs were blocked by robots.txt. AnkiHub's founders are "Nick (The AnKing, a resident physician)" and Andrew Sanchez, and a commenter asked whether free content would move behind a paywall; AnkiHub's reply did not address it — [Anki forums, Anki's Growing Up](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610.md)

**OER community**
- WeBWorK Open Problem Library: new problems go in `Contrib` where authors keep control and are automatically considered for the library; the OPL Editorial Board reviews them while copies sit in `Pending`; the curated `OpenProblemLibrary` is what the Library Browser shows; README badge shows CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; 10,391 commits, 13 open pull requests; problem count not stated — [OPL repository](https://github.com/openwebwork/webwork-open-problem-library)
- OpenStax (Oct 2025): 43.3M learners in 169 countries, over 80 titles, used at 72% of US colleges, "over $3 billion" in student savings (headline) versus "nearly $3.4 billion" (body), a figure conflict inside one article; optional paid add-ons like Assignable (nearly 900 US institutions) are priced "to help sustain the organization's nonprofit mission" while textbooks stay free — [Rice University news](https://news.rice.edu/news/2025/openstax-surpasses-3b-student-savings-grows-beyond-textbooks)
- LibreTexts: 500M+ page views since 2008 (UC Davis article, undated), a $1M California grant, and a consortium of colleges and universities (the article names UC Davis, University of Michigan, University of Kansas, DePauw University and others); quality control and the community contribution model are not described in the article — [UC Davis Letters & Science](https://lettersandscience.ucdavis.edu/blog/free-textbooks-site-passes-500m-views). A search-result headline reports that LibreTexts was awarded a $5 million federal OER (Open Textbook Pilot) grant in 2018; I did not fetch that page — [Infodocket](https://infodocket.com/2018/10/02/oeropen-textbooks-libretexts-awarded-5-million-federal-oer-grant)
- US Department of Education rule (2 CFR 3474.20): copyrightable deliverables created with competitive-grant funds (competitions announced after Feb 21, 2017) must carry a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual, irrevocable open license; any open license meeting the requirements is allowed, "including one limited to noncommercial use"; a dissemination plan is required — [2 CFR 3474.20 text](https://www.customsmobile.com/regulations/title2_chapterXXXIV_part3474_section3474.20)
- The Open Definition requires licenses to allow redistribution including sale and use for any purpose, so NonCommercial licenses do not qualify as "open" — [Open Definition 2.1](https://opendefinition.org/od/2.1/en/)

**Local constraints already in the spec**: packs contain no executable code (PACK-3), the pack builder refuses cards whose license is not on an allowlist that includes CC BY-NC and BY-NC-SA (PACK-7), shipped packs are released as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (PACK-9), and every key must grade itself correct and every stored wrong answer must fail (MATH-13) — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md).

### Inferences
- A safe contribution pipeline for a one-person project can reuse what already exists: (1) a documented pack/card-file format and a stand-alone `pack lint` command that runs the build-time checks (schema, license allowlist, provenance, KaTeX strict render, self-grading keys, all `wrong` answers fail, no code) so a teacher gets exact line-level errors before submitting; (2) tiers shown in the app and registry: Official (pipeline-verified), Reviewed community (lint passes plus two named human reviews), Unreviewed community (lint passes only, clearly marked), Personal (never shared; PACK-8); (3) a static registry page (generated from files, no accounts needed) listing pack, version, license, provenance, tier, and a report link.
- Ratings should be harder to fake than AnkiWeb's: tie any rating to an app-generated "usage receipt" (for example 50+ attempts on that pack) and show objective signals beside stars (reports per 1,000 attempts, key-disputes confirmed). This is a design suggestion, not something I found in use.
- Copyright hygiene matters more than quality hygiene for teachers' packs: require a license choice from a short list, a "this is my own work or I hold the license" attestation (the AnkiWeb wording is a precedent), a similarity check against known commercial banks (the spec already plans one for clean-room decks), and a takedown path with a published contact.
- License-strategy tension: CC BY-NC-SA suits the owner's source mix but is not "open" by the Open Definition, excludes commercial reuse by tutoring services and publishers, and the one big US grant-rule found still permits NC; shipping AI-original or owner-written content under CC BY or CC BY-SA, and keeping NC only on pack parts inherited from NC sources, would widen reuse and funding eligibility. That tradeoff is a decision for the owner, not a research fact.
- The community phase needs a public forge for submissions and pack hosting; the spec's "no GitHub" decision will need revisiting or replacing with another public forge before contributions can be accepted (alternatives were not researched here).

### Gaps
- Total number of AnkiWeb shared decks and the share that are copyright-clean could not be retrieved (the AnkiWeb pages returned no text); "licensing chaos" is evidenced only by forum threads and the ToS/takedown mechanics, not by a measured count.
- AnKing's actual internal review process (who reviews what, how errors are caught) is not documented in anything I could read; only AnkiHub's marketing claims.
- OpenStax's errata process and LibreTexts' quality control were not found.
- No empirical study of rating manipulation or of review-gate effects on pack quality was found.

## 5. Translation and localization: Weblate, Crowdin, Anki's model, and what localizing math content takes

### Takeaway
Free hosted translation platforms exist for public open-source projects (Weblate's Libre plan up to 160,000 hosted strings; Crowdin free for qualifying open-source projects), and Anki runs a lightweight Fluent-based interface with account-by-request and BSD-licensed translations. Localizing math content is harder than translating UI: decimal marks, function names and set-symbol conventions differ by country, so the answer checker and renderer need per-locale notation profiles, and gaokao content needs a native Chinese reviewer.

### Cited Findings
- Anki translations are made in Anki's own web interface (i18n.ankiweb.net); translators request an account by private message on the support site, name their languages, and agree to license translations under the 3-clause BSD license — [Anki translator intro](https://docs.ankiweb.net/translators/intro.md). Core strings use Fluent, with plural boxes per language, a Locales tab to see how other languages handled a string, and each new Anki beta includes the latest translations so translators can test; no review process or language count is stated — [Anki translators, core](https://docs.ankiweb.net/translators/anki/core.md)
- Hosted Weblate's free "Libre" plan: 160,000 hosted strings, unlimited languages, projects and components, "only for public projects", apply at hosted.weblate.org/hosting/; the same tier costs EUR 114/month or EUR 1,140/year for non-libre use — [Weblate hosting](https://weblate.org/en/hosting/)
- Crowdin is free for open-source projects that use an open-source-initiative-approved license, have public source, no commercial products tied to the project, an active community and regular releases, a project lead with at least three months on the project; participants join Crowdin's beta group and contribute to its global translation memory — [Crowdin open-source request](https://crowdin.com/page/open-source-project-setup-request)
- Notation differences are real but bounded: decimal point vs comma (the German standard DIN makes the comma official); French and Russian texts write binomial coefficients as C with indices; Spanish texts use "sen" for sine; gcd is gcd/ggT/ggd/pgcd; whether the natural numbers include zero varies by culture, with N+ or N* used to exclude it; the authors conclude diversity is enough to disorient readers but not enough to require software to adapt to every culture, and the paper does not cover China, interval notation or logarithm names — [Notations Around the World: Census and Exploitation (arXiv 1004.5165)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1004.5165)
- GeoGebra's translation and deployment are coordinated by the nonprofit International GeoGebra Institute; Moodle is translated into over 100 languages — [Wikipedia, GeoGebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGebra); [Wikipedia, Moodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moodle)
- Chinese courts have recognized copyright in standardized test questions in at least one settled GMAT case (mediation, RMB 520,000 paid by a test-prep seller; GMAC's own press release, not the court text) — [GMAC](https://www.gmac.com/why-gmac/gmac-news/gmac-press-releases/beijing-court-upholds-copyright-protection-for-gmat-exam). The spec lists "a check of which past papers may be redistributed" as an unresolved step for gaokao content and already plans AI-original Gaokao-style problems verified by blind solve, code solve and adversarial audit — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md)
- The spec's pipeline already has an acceptance path for translated problems (blind solve of the English version must reproduce the official answer; marked translated with unofficial-solution labels) and per-card provenance fields (PACK-4) — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md)

### Inferences
- Cheapest localization architecture: externalize every UI string now (ICU/Fluent-style with plural rules), format numbers and dates through `Intl` at render time, separate each problem's mathematical structure (LaTeX/MathJSON, keys, checker data) from its natural-language text so a translation changes only strings, and add a per-locale "notation profile" used by both the renderer and the answer checker (accepting locale-typical function names and set symbols, and decimal comma on input). This keeps a translated card from needing new answer keys.
- Platform choice: Weblate's Libre plan or Crowdin's OSS program both need a public project; both therefore depend on the open-license and public-forge decisions in section 4. Anki's account-by-request model is the lowest overhead for the first 2-3 languages.
- Priority order for translation, given reach data: Chinese (gaokao, 12.9M candidates) first, but only with a native-speaker reviewer and a human check of notation on a sample of real gaokao papers; then languages where Android phones dominate (Hindi and other Indian languages need UI strings only if packs are English-language STEM problems; a UI-only translation is far cheaper than content translation).
- AI-assisted translation of problem text can be verified mechanically for answer equality (the blind-solve gate), but terminology and notation conventions (e.g., how a Chinese textbook states a set or a combination) need human review; do not treat the machine gate as sufficient for Chinese users.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve any source on Chinese-specific mathematical notation (the Chinese national standard GB 3102.11 page did not load); specific differences such as how Chinese textbooks write positive-integer sets, complements, combinations and logarithms are therefore unverified here and should be checked against real gaokao papers.
- Anki's number of supported languages, and any quality-review step in its translation flow, are not stated in the docs I read.
- Weblate and Crowdin eligibility for content under NonCommercial licenses was not checked (Crowdin says "open-source initiative approved license").
- Whether any gaokao past papers may be legally redistributed was not resolved; the only legal evidence found concerns foreign standardized tests.

## 6. Sustainability of free open-source education software, governance, and the bus factor

### Takeaway
Nearly every durable free education tool has a paid layer, a funding body or an institution behind it: Anki (paid iOS app, now stewarded by AnkiHub), Khan Academy ($90M in 2025 contributions), Desmos (public-benefit corporation funded by 75+ partner organizations), PrairieLearn and Moodle (paid hosting and partner royalties), OpenStax (optional paid tools) and LibreTexts (grants). Counter-examples show the risk of a single owner (GeoGebra's parent in insolvency; Anki's own creator handing over in 2026). A solo, zero-revenue project should keep running costs near zero, document succession, and seek small grants whose rules it can actually meet.

### Cited Findings
**Anki and AnkiDroid**
- On Feb 2, 2026 Anki's creator announced he is gradually handing business operations and open-source stewardship to AnkiHub; both are described as self-funded and profitable with no outside investors; no Anki pricing changes planned; core code to remain open source; AnkiDroid "will remain an open-source, self-governed project"; "the ecosystem will no longer be in jeopardy if I'm one day hit by a bus"; a formal governance model was promised "in the coming weeks" with an advisory board "being explored" — [Anki forums, Anki's Growing Up](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610.md)
- June 16, 2026 update: version 26.05 is the first stable release as a consolidated team; the post does not mention funding or governance outcomes — [Anki Team update June 2026](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/updates-from-the-anki-team-june-2026/70120/1.md)
- AnkiDroid's Open Collective: $216,022.79 raised in total, $136,420.46 disbursed, estimated annual budget $71,793.22, 8,552 contributors; sponsor tiers pay for developer hours ($150/month = 10 hours, $300/month = 20 hours); a 2025 year-end update says contributions let maintainers "cut down on our dayjobs" — [AnkiDroid Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/ankidroid)

**Other programs**
- Khan Academy (nonprofit): fiscal year ending June 2025 revenue $117.5M (contributions $90.4M, program service revenue $22.2M), expenses $87.5M, net assets $152.2M; calendar 2023 revenue $98.3M (contributions $84.2M); the "June 2024" row ($28.1M revenue) appears to be a short transition period (my inference from the row sequence) — [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/261544963)
- Desmos: Amplify bought the curriculum business on May 18, 2022; the calculators moved to Desmos Studio, a public benefit corporation, to stay free for teachers and students and be licensed to companies; its partnerships with 75+ organizations were said to fund ongoing R&D — [Amplify announcement](https://amplify.com/news/amplify-acquires-desmos-curriculum-to-build-the-future-of-math-instruction-desmos-calculators-to-remain-independent-and-free-to-all/); Desmos Studio's site metadata claims use by over 100 million people — [Desmos about](https://desmos.com/about)
- GeoGebra: freeware under a non-commercial license (some GPL components); funded by a Kickstarter for mobile apps, then sold to Byju's for about $100M in Dec 2021; Byju's US arm filed Chapter 11 on Feb 1, 2024; the nonprofit International GeoGebra Institute coordinates development and translation — [Wikipedia, GeoGebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGebra). "Investment in Geogebra GmbH" appears among indicative assets in Byju's Indian insolvency process (list dated Oct 5, 2025); no valuation given — [Insolvency Tracker](https://insolvencytracker.in/2025/10/05/list-of-byjus-assets-up-for-sale-under-insolvency-resolution-process/). I did not find a confirmed 2026 owner.
- PrairieLearn began as open-source software at the University of Illinois and "will remain that way forever"; PrairieLearn, Inc. sells paid hosting whose subscriptions fund sysadmins, maintenance and support — [PrairieLearn about](https://prairielearn.com/about)
- Moodle HQ is financially supported by 80 Moodle Partner companies through royalties (percentage not stated on the page); GPLv3+; over 100 languages; stable 5.3 dated Oct 5, 2026 — [Wikipedia, Moodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moodle)
- Mnemosyne: the project's website (mnemosyne-proj.org) displayed a "site suspended" hosting notice when I fetched it; its development status is unknown — [Mnemosyne site](https://mnemosyne-proj.org/)

**Funding routes and constraints**
- NLnet NGI Zero: first grants of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000; the form's applicant types include Individual and Collective (without legal entity) as well as organizations; next deadline Nov 3, 2026 12:00 CET; software, hardware and content must be released under open licences; grants are "primarily for technical development"; EU-supported funds require a "strong European dimension" (applicants mostly from Horizon Europe-eligible countries); "Proposals and projects should be made by humans, not AI", and AI prompts and logs must be kept and submitted if AI was used — [NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/propose/)
- Learning Engineering Tools Competition (2027 cycle): over $4.5M, prizes of $50,000 (Catalyst, early stage), $150,000 (Growth) and $300,000 (Transform, 10,000+ users), emphasis on evidence, safety and real-world use; Phase I abstracts were due Oct 13, 2026 (already passed), Phase II Jan 21, 2027, winners June 2027; the article does not say whether individuals can apply and mentions no open-source requirement; an impact study runs across 2027 and 2028 — [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/2027-tools-competition-opens-with-45m-for-evidence-based-edtech-and-ai)
- Open Source Collective is a 501(c)(6) fiscal host for a network of 3,000+ projects; contributions to hosted collectives are not tax-deductible charitable gifts; fee terms were not in the fetched text — [Open Source Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensource)
- Apple fee waiver and Microsoft/SignPath items in section 3 also function as zero-cost sustainability levers.

**Bus factor and maintainer evidence**
- Across 133 popular GitHub projects, about 65% have a truck factor of 2 or lower (the minimum number of developers whose loss would cripple the project) — [Avelino et al., arXiv 1604.06766](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06766)
- Tidelift's 2024 maintainer survey (400 respondents, via SD Times): 60% of maintainers are unpaid and 60% have quit or considered quitting — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/open-source/the-state-of-open-source-maintainers/)

### Inferences
- Models that map to this project: (a) "free core, paid services for institutions" (PrairieLearn hosting, Moodle partners, AnkiMobile volume licences, OpenStax Assignable) — but it conflicts with the owner's no-revenue intent and with Apple's fee-waiver condition of not selling digital goods; (b) donations/fiscal host (AnkiDroid shows what a very large volunteer app raises: about $72k/year budget across 8,552 contributors; a new app should expect far less); (c) grants (NLnet, Tools Competition, ED-style OER grants), each with a likely blocker for this project today: NLnet's human-authorship rule and European-dimension rule; the Tools Competition's evidence emphasis and passed Phase I deadline; for ED-style OER grants I researched only the licensing rule, not eligibility; (d) institutional adoption (a college or teacher cohort hosts packs and gives the project a home).
- Practical bus-factor controls for a solo, agent-built project: keep infrastructure cost at about zero (static hosting, no accounts, no sync server), keep the ADR/AGENTS.md/test-suite discipline the spec already has, publish a "if the maintainer stops" note and escrow release credentials, domains and signing keys with a trusted second person or fiscal host, mirror the repo and releases in at least two places, and recruit one co-maintainer before the community phase (Anki's own fix was to add an institutional steward).
- Governance sequencing: the Anki case suggests formal governance (advisory board, steward organization) is a response to scale and bus-factor risk, not a prerequisite; for a young project a short written charter (license, contribution rules, succession, content-quality policy) is enough.

### Gaps
- Khan Academy's operating costs per learner, Desmos Studio's revenue, GeoGebra's current owner and license changes, Moodle's royalty percentage and PrairieLearn's scale were not found.
- Mnemosyne's repository activity and maintainer status were not checked.
- No foundation list specific to free STEM practice tools was compiled beyond NLnet and the Tools Competition; eligibility of an individual student applicant for the Tools Competition and for ED grants is unconfirmed.
- Fee terms for Open Source Collective and for other fiscal hosts were not retrieved.

## 7. How to credibly show a learning app is better: ESSA tiers, WWC standards, small studies, comparison designs, ethics, and what Duolingo, Khan Academy and Math Academy publish

### Takeaway
A solo project can reach ESSA Tier 4 immediately (logic model plus a published evaluation plan) and Tier 3 with controlled correlational analyses, but Tier 1-2 claims need 350+ participants across multiple sites. Credible small-N evidence comes from preregistered within-student comparisons and single-case designs scored on delayed, non-aligned outcomes. Competitor "efficacy" claims vary from randomized trials (ASSISTments and interleaved practice, rated by Evidence for ESSA or WWC; Khan Academy's listed RCTs, reported on its own promotional page) to company-run correlational and quasi-experimental analyses (Khan, Duolingo) to anecdotes (Math Academy).

### Cited Findings
**Standards**
- ESSA tiers (CADRE handout; table layout was garbled so some cells are inferred): Tier 1 Strong: well-designed experimental study meeting WWC standards without reservations, statistically significant favorable effect, sample of 350+ participants, multisite, overlapping population and setting; Tier 2 Moderate: well-designed quasi-experiment (or experiment with high attrition) meeting WWC with reservations, same significance, 350+ and multisite; Tier 3 Promising: well-designed correlational study with statistical controls for selection bias; Tier 4 Demonstrates a Rationale: a well-specified logic model grounded in rigorous research plus a planned or ongoing evaluation — [CADRE ESSA Evidence Tier Criteria handout](https://www.cadrek12.org/sites/default/files/ESSA%20Evidence%20Tier%20Criteria%20Handout.pdf). The ed.gov guidance PDF failed to load.
- WWC Handbook v5.0: three ratings (Meets Without Reservations, Meets With Reservations, Does Not Meet); RCTs, regression-discontinuity and single-case designs can earn the top rating while quasi-experiments top out at With Reservations; outcomes need face validity, reliability, no over-alignment with the intervention and consistent collection; a confound affecting all findings (for example one unit per condition, or a time period aligned with one condition) means Does Not Meet; attrition is judged by an algorithm (Deke and Chiang, 2017) and concerning attrition caps the rating at With Reservations (the specific boundaries are in Appendix C, which the fetched excerpt did not include); for single-case designs the initial baseline needs at least six data points for the top rating under v5.0 — [WWC Handbook v5.0](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/referenceresources/Final_WWC-HandbookVer5.0-0-508.pdf)
- WWC quasi-experiment FAQ: baseline differences under 0.05 SD need no adjustment, between 0.05 and 0.25 SD need statistical controls, above 0.25 SD fail equivalence; WWC sets no minimum sample size but requires at least two units (classrooms, teachers, schools) per condition — [WWC, Designing Quasi-Experiments](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/document/256)
- Federal capacity has shrunk: in 2025 IES contracts were cut, 10 Regional Education Labs lost $300M+ in contracts, IES headcount fell to under 20, and the WWC had reduced support with Practice Guides at risk (July 2025 report; not a statement that WWC closed) — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/back-to-the-dark-ages-education-research-staggered-by-trump-cuts/)

**What competitors and comparators publish**
- Khan Academy's own evidence page lists RCTs (Hamilton County TN, about 6,900 students; Oreopoulos et al. 2024, about 11,000 students, 0.12-0.22 SD; an India trial of about 5,500 students with a support arm, 0.44-0.47 SD), quasi-experiments (about 200K students, MAP Growth gains 0.09-0.18 SD for +30 min/week or +60 skills) and correlational studies; it is promotional and mixes in-house and independent authors, and notes the AI tutor contributed little in the Hamilton trial — [Khan Academy blog, evidence](https://blog.khanacademy.org/multiple-studies-show-khan-academy-drives-learning-gains-evidence-for-our-platforms-effectiveness)
- Khan's Nov 2024 results: Study 1 (about 350K grade 3-8 students) is a cross-sectional comparison of usage levels in which 30+ minutes/week is associated with about 20% greater-than-expected MAP Growth gains, effect size 0.36, and only about 9% of students reach that dose (the page describes no controls beyond the "greater-than-expected" framing); Study 2 compares each of about 221K students with their own prior year (described as quasi-experimental), with each additional skill practiced to proficient or mastered associated with about 0.5 percentage points of gain; the authors call the longitudinal design "rigorous" and "relatively rare" but say only an RCT is more rigorous, and the page has no limitations section — [Khan Academy blog, Nov 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18194). A January 2026 PNAS paper (about 200K students) reports a positive usage-MAP Growth relationship with no effect size in the company's summary and average use of 10-15 minutes/week — [Khan Academy blog on PNAS study](https://blog.khanacademy.org/national-study-in-top-journal-finds-khan-academy-learning-gains-after-accounting-for-key-unmeasured-factors/)
- ASSISTments (free, WPI-developed): Evidence for ESSA rates it Strong on 2 school-level randomized studies (Maine, 43 schools, +0.18; North Carolina replication, 63 schools and about 6,000 students, +0.10), 8,841 students total, average +0.13; the page does not state evaluator independence — [Evidence for ESSA](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=630)
- Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig and Cheung (2020), cluster RCT of interleaved math practice with grade 7 students in Florida middle schools, rated Meets WWC Standards Without Reservations; main study 787 students over 43 days (intervention mean 62.17 vs comparison 37.60, improvement index 31) and a pilot of 83 students over 35 days (improvement index 34) — [WWC study review](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/88770)
- Duolingo: its efficacy page did not render for me. Jiang et al. (2021, Foreign Language Annals): 225 adult learners on beginning Spanish/French courses reached scores "comparable with" fourth-semester university students (Intermediate Low reading, Novice High listening); one author is listed at Duolingo, no funding statement was visible — [NAU repository entry](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/evaluating-the-reading-and-listening-outcomes-of-beginning-level-/). Settles and Meeder (ACL 2016) report two live A/B tests: Experiment I (half-life regression vs a Leitner-style scheduler) left overall activity and new lessons essentially flat (+0.3%) and cut practice sessions by 7.3% (significant), yet HLR was deployed on student feedback; Experiment II (HLR without lexeme features compared with HLR, about 3.3 million students) raised daily retention 12.0% for any activity, which is the source of the "12%" figure in the abstract and which the authors attribute to avoiding overfitting on lexeme features. So the headline 12% compares two HLR variants, not HLR against the earlier scheduler. They also released their data and code — [ACL 2016 paper](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- Math Academy: the "success stories" page is a self-selected "living document" of anecdotes with no controlled studies, no comparison group and no independent evaluation; pass rates come without counts ("most" students who took AP Calc BC in eighth grade passed), exam samples are tiny (2019-20: one independent learner who earned a 5; 2021-22: four unaffiliated students, three of whom scored a 5 and one a 4), the claim of being "4x as efficient as traditional in-person classes" in 2021-22 has no sample, measures or source, and the original school cohort was selected at or above the 90th percentile on a placement exam; the author built the software and publishes the page on his own site; The Math Academy Way is a 400+ page working draft that summarizes research papers and is the author's own synthesis, not new trial data — [Justin Skycak, Math Academy success stories](https://justinmath.com/math-academy-success-stories/)

**Local plan already in the spec**
- The spec's validation plan (DEV-16) calls for strict-label true retention with Wilson intervals, predicted-against-actual score for every real exam, and "one preregistered experiment per semester: written down before the semester starts, randomized at the skill level, with at least about 1,100 rated reviews", with the adaptive learning gate (ENG-2) as the first question and holding items back from practice kept optional because it "costs a student whose grades matter"; its usability plan (DEV-15) is three think-aloud rounds of about five users each ("three classmates plus the owner is enough"), with SUS reported as a range; both sit in milestone M13, after 1.1 — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md)

**Ethics**
- The US Common Rule exempts research in established educational settings involving normal educational practices not likely to adversely impact students' opportunity to learn, explicitly including comparisons of instructional techniques and curricula (45 CFR 46.104(d)(1)); educational tests, surveys and interviews are exempt if identities are not recorded, disclosure would not expose subjects to harm, or an IRB performs limited review (d)(2) — [eCFR 45 CFR 46.104](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.104)

### Inferences
- Sample-size arithmetic (my calculation, 80% power, two-sided alpha .05, t-based): to detect d=0.5 you need 64 per group (128 total) in a between-group trial or 34 students in a within-student paired design; d=0.3 needs 176 per group (352 total) or 90 paired; d=0.2 needs 394 per group. With 15 students per group the minimum detectable effect is about d=1.06; with 20 per group 0.91; 30 per group 0.74; a paired design with 20 students detects about dz=0.66 and with 30 about 0.53. If whole classrooms are assigned, typical design effects (intraclass correlation 0.10-0.20, classes of 20-30) are 2.9-6.8 times larger, which is why class-level trials need many classes. These figures show why a handful of classmates cannot support Tier 1-2 claims, and ESSA's 350+/multisite bar matches that.
- Evidence ladder for a solo project: (Tier 4, now) publish a logic model and a preregistered evaluation plan on a public registry; (Tier 3) opt-in exported usage logs analysed with controls, in the style of Khan's within-student comparisons, and the app's own validation goals (realized retention inside the Wilson interval of target, per the spec) as internal verification; (Tier 1-2, later) partner with teachers or a research group for a multi-site trial of 350+ students, as ASSISTments did.
- Designs that work at small N: (1) skill-level micro-randomization within each student (randomly assign comparable skills to FSRS scheduling vs a fixed-interval or massed baseline, or to "fresh problem" vs "same problem" review) gives hundreds of student-skill pairs from 20-40 classmates and can answer scheduler questions causally, echoing Duolingo's A/B approach (the spec's DEV-16 already calls for one preregistered skill-level randomized experiment per semester for the owner alone, so this would extend an existing plan by pooling opt-in logs from several students); (2) counterbalanced crossover vs Anki on the same topics (each student uses the app for topic A and Anki or their usual method for topic B, order randomized, time-on-task logged and matched); (3) single-case multiple-baseline across students or skills, with at least six baseline points per the WWC v5.0 text.
- Comparison-design cautions: use delayed (1-4 weeks) tests with fresh, independently written problems, not the app's own bank (WWC "over-alignment" rule); blind the grader; match time on task because mastery systems often win by simply adding practice; report all preregistered outcomes with confidence intervals; and treat vendor-hosted comparisons (Khan, Duolingo) as a template for transparency, not for causal strength.
- Ethics for peers: do not self-certify exemption; ask the college's research office or IRB whether a student-run classmate study is research under the Common Rule and whether (d)(1)/(d)(2) apply; make participation voluntary and unrelated to grades, get written consent, keep data pseudonymous and local until a participant chooses to export it, allow withdrawal, and avoid recruiting minors without guardian consent and institutional approval. The offline, no-telemetry design is an ethics advantage because data leaves a device only by participant action.
- What counts as "better" should be pre-specified on learning, not engagement: delayed-test performance and exam outcomes first; retention accuracy against target and time-to-mastery second; engagement only as context.

## 8. Roadmap synthesis: what to do, in what order, with no budget

### Takeaway
Sequence the work so every step is cheap, reversible and removes a hard blocker for the next: keep 1.0 and 1.1 as planned, make a handful of low-cost decisions now (licenses, public forge, string and notation externalization, distribution ADR, study-log export), then ship a web/PWA build as the single biggest reach step, add Windows through the free Microsoft Store route, and run a preregistered small study in parallel to build credibility.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence behind this ordering is cited in sections 1-7: Mac is about 13% of US, 3% of China and 1% of India page views; Windows is 92% of China's and 81% of India's desktop page views; mobile is 59% worldwide and 77% in India; managed school Chromebooks block desktop installs; Apple's fee waiver excludes individuals; Microsoft Store registration is free for individuals; Pyodide plus SymPy is roughly 17 MB raw; Weblate and Crowdin free tiers and SignPath require public/open projects; NLnet requires human authorship and a European dimension; ESSA Tier 1-2 needs 350+ multisite participants.
- The existing spec already contains the enabling pieces: data-only packs (PACK-3), license allowlist and provenance (PACK-4/7), self-verifying keys (MATH-13), a UI-free core enforced by import-linter (ARC-1), a one-file shell adapter, usability rounds with classmates (DEV-15), validation goals including realized-vs-target retention, and a preregistered per-semester skill-level randomized experiment (DEV-16) — [spec.md](/docs/spec.md).

### Inferences
**Now, during 1.0/1.1 (zero cost, mostly decisions)**
1. Decide the app's source license (an OSI-approved license is needed for SignPath free signing and Crowdin's OSS program, and NLnet requires open licences) and the pack license strategy (consider CC BY or CC BY-SA for original content; keep NC only where inherited), and fix the `fsrs-rs-python` license issue the spec already flags.
2. Decide the public forge for code, issues and contributions (the spec's "no GitHub" rule blocks community pull requests and free CI), or choose an alternative public forge (alternatives not researched here).
3. Externalize UI strings and define the per-locale notation profile interface (renderer plus checker). The card format was already frozen at the end of M2 (M0-M6 are built), so locale fields (a language tag per pack or card, an optional notation profile) would need a versioned, backward-compatible extension of the pack `format` field rather than a pre-freeze change.

**After 1.1 (target: first half of 2027)**
